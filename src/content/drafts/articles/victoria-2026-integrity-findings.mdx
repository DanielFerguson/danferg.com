Some criticisms of this government are supported by serious findings. I don’t need to exaggerate them to take them seriously, and the distinctions matter. An opposition allegation starts a question. An investigation may substantiate misconduct, identify corruption risks, or make a formal corrupt-conduct finding. A criminal charge is separate again, and a conviction is separate from that. Most of the heat in this campaign comes from blurring those lines in one direction or the other, which is why this piece in [the guide](/articles/victoria-election-2026) puts each case on the same scale.

<h2 id="watts-and-daintree" tabindex="-1">Operations Watts and Daintree</h2>

Operation Watts, reported in 2022, is the joint IBAC–Ombudsman investigation that found widespread misuse of public resources for factional purposes: publicly funded staff doing party work, nepotism, bullying. It examined conduct involving former ministers Adem Somyurek and Marlene Kairouz. Investigators called the conduct egregious but couldn’t recommend prosecution given the difficulties of proof and the state of the law, and found no evidence that community grants paid party membership fees <Cite k="watts-report" /> <Tag kind="independent" />. Those limits don’t make the conduct acceptable.

Operation Daintree, 2023, examined a $1.2 million health training contract awarded to an entity linked to the Health Workers Union. IBAC found improper political influence and failures of public duty in the procurement, and criticised the oversight. It did not make a corrupt-conduct finding, because Victorian law reserves that term for conduct that would be a crime <Cite k="daintree-ibac-annual" /> <Tag kind="independent" />.

<h2 id="richmond" tabindex="-1">Operation Richmond</h2>

Richmond landed on 5 August this year and concerns 2016. IBAC found Andrews and senior figures bypassed proper processes in negotiations with the United Firefighters Union, excluding the responsible minister, the CFA and the department, and giving union secretary Peter Marshall extraordinary influence. It made no corrupt-conduct finding, and the blackmail allegation that prompted the investigation was not substantiated <Cite k="richmond-abc" /> <Tag kind="independent" />. The conduct was Andrews’s; the report landed on Carroll’s desk, and he accepted its recommendations.

<h2 id="the-games" tabindex="-1">The Commonwealth Games</h2>

The withdrawal cost more than $589 million, including a $380 million settlement, for an event that never happened. The Auditor-General found the original business case understated costs and overstated benefits, and that agencies failed to give frank and timely advice. The government’s public claim that hosting would have cost $6.9 billion was also overstated; the audit put a fair figure closer to $5.9 billion <Cite k="games-audit" /> <Tag kind="independent" />. This is a documented failure of planning and administration without anyone needing to call it criminal.

<h2 id="pandemic-governance" tabindex="-1">Pandemic governance</h2>

Two findings I keep separate. The hotel quarantine inquiry documented systemic failures of responsibility and oversight <Cite k="hotel-quarantine" />; WorkSafe’s charges over the program were later withdrawn <Cite k="hq-charges-dropped" />, and a $125 million class-action settlement was agreed this March without an admission <Cite k="hq-settlement" />. Separately, the Ombudsman found the immediate lockdown of the public housing towers breached human rights, while accepting that a temporary lockdown was warranted; the government disputed the legal conclusion <Cite k="tower-lockdown" /> <Tag kind="independent" />.

<h2 id="the-public-service" tabindex="-1">The public service</h2>

In 2023 the Ombudsman found creeping politicisation of the public service and damaging secrecy, while not substantiating the specific allegation that it had been stacked with party operatives <Cite k="ombudsman-politicisation" /> <Tag kind="independent" />. Both halves belong in an honest account.

<h2 id="sandon-and-the-cfmeu" tabindex="-1">Sandon and the CFMEU review</h2>

Two more need careful attribution. Operation Sandon found councillors at the City of Casey advancing a developer’s interests for money, and identified weak planning and lobbying controls at state level; IBAC’s own headline was “serious corruption risks” <Cite k="sandon-release" />. A former councillor and a consultant were charged in September 2025, the one case here that has reached a court, and charges aren’t convictions <Cite k="sandon-charges" />. None of it is a finding that a premier was corrupt.

The Watson review of the Victorian CFMEU, commissioned by the union’s administrator and made public in February, documented corruption, intimidation and government inaction on the Big Build <Cite k="watson-review" />. It deserves attention. It’s a private review rather than an IBAC finding or a judgment, and the $15 billion figure attached to it is an estimate the administrator himself redacted as untested. The royal commission now examining the sector reports in August 2027 <Cite k="abc-kourakis" />.

<h2 id="what-a-new-leader-has-to-show" tabindex="-1">What a new leader has to show</h2>

Taken together, these give me substantial reasons to question how power has been exercised. A new leader needs to show what changes in practice: independence of advice, procurement, and openness to scrutiny. Carroll’s first weeks are mixed on that. He called the royal commission on day one and accepted Richmond’s recommendations. He also scrapped a 1% levy on public transport fares that the government had introduced in January 2025 without public disclosure, which only came to light through the Auditor-General in late August <Cite k="abc-srl" />. The same week, the government claimed $1 billion in Suburban Rail Loop savings that the Auditor-General pointed out had never been in the project’s estimate. Openness to scrutiny will be judged on more than one week.

The credit side of this ledger is in [Labor’s record](/articles/victoria-2026-labor-record). What the alternatives would do differently starts with [roads and emergency services](/articles/victoria-2026-roads-emergency-services).
