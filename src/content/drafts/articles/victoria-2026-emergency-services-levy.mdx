<h2 id="what-the-levy-is" tabindex="-1">What the levy is</h2>

The Emergency Services and Volunteers Fund levy replaced the Fire Services Property Levy on 1 July 2025 and funds a wider set of agencies <Cite k="sro-esvf" /> <Tag kind="delivered" />. Councils collect it through the rates notice, but the state sets it. It has two parts: a fixed charge and a variable charge on capital improved value, which is land plus buildings and improvements. Classification follows land use, not postcode; a country address alone doesn’t make a property primary production <Cite k="sro-calc" />.

<h2 id="why-the-numbers-differ" tabindex="-1">Why people remember such different numbers</h2>

The original proposal put the farm rate at 83 cents per $1,000 of value, up from 28.7 cents. On 15 May 2025 the government cut that to 71.8 cents <Cite k="esvf-may-amendment" />. On 30 May it froze the farm rate at 28.7 cents for 2025–26 as drought relief <Cite k="esvf-drought-freeze" />. December’s Budget Update extended the relief <Cite k="budget-update-dec-2025" />, the current 2026–27 schedule still shows 28.7 cents <Cite k="sro-current-rates" />, and regional press reads the May 2026 budget papers as carrying the freeze through to mid-2030 <Cite k="acm-freeze-2030" />. The 189% increase people quote was real, as a proposal. It has never appeared on a bill.

So the honest comparison has several versions in it. The calculator shows all of them for a property of your choosing <Tag kind="author" />:

For the $1 million example I started with, a farm’s levy rose 2.7% across the two years, from $554 to $569, while a home of the same value rose 42.5%, from $219 to $312. The farm still pays about 82% more than the equally valued home in 2026–27. That’s a comparison between land uses in one year. It’s a different thing from the farm’s own increase, and different again from the original proposal. I shouldn’t turn any of them into “every country household now pays 100 to 200% more than Melbourne”.

<h2 id="rebates" tabindex="-1">The volunteer rebate</h2>

Eligible CFA, VICSES and Shepparton Search and Rescue volunteers can claim a rebate on a principal home or qualifying farm. The farm cap is $3,152 in 2026–27, which covers a farm valued at $10 million. Eligibility carries service requirements and an annual application; community fundraising alone doesn’t qualify anyone; and you can apply before or after paying the notice <Cite k="volunteer-rebate" />.

<h2 id="the-question-that-remains" tabindex="-1">The question that remains</h2>

Freezing a variable rate doesn’t freeze the bill: fixed charges index and valuations move. And temporary relief settles nothing about the design. My remaining question is whether property value is a fair basis for sharing this cost when a valuable working property can produce a modest income. The Coalition says it would scrap the levy and return to the old Fire Services Property Levy <Cite k="abc-cfa-rally" />, which was also charged on property value, so the question doesn’t go away under the main alternative either. Any alternative still has to explain how reliable emergency services get funded. That comparison is the subject of [the next article on roads and emergency services](/articles/victoria-2026-roads-emergency-services); the government’s wider record is in [Labor’s record](/articles/victoria-2026-labor-record).
