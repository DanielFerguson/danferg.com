<h2 id="what-the-audit-found" tabindex="-1">What the Auditor-General found</h2>

On 9 September the Auditor-General tabled a report on how the state maintains its roads <Cite k="vago-roads" />. Statewide, the share of state roads rated poor or very poor for remaining useful life rose from about 39% in 2021 to about 48% in 2024. In regional Victoria it was 50.38% <Tag kind="independent" />. Half.

<VictoriaRoadsComparison />

That measure estimates structural life: “poor” means five to fifteen years of life left in the pavement, “very poor” under five. It’s evidence of a maintenance problem growing faster than it’s being fixed, not a declaration that half the roads are unsafe today.

The state manages arterial roads and freeways, a little over 23,000 km, and 82% of that network is regional. Councils manage most local roads, and toll roads have their own operators. Before blaming anyone for a particular stretch, the Department of Transport and Planning’s map says whose it is <Cite k="dtp-road-map" />.

<h2 id="why-spending-hasnt-fixed-it" tabindex="-1">Why more spending hasn’t fixed it</h2>

The department told the government that funding wasn’t sufficient and that maintenance had become reactive: potholes and safety patches rather than resurfacing that lasts. The audit found the department doesn’t always document why it prioritises one site over another, and that about one in four routine works inspected didn’t meet the standard. The gap between defects recorded and defects repaired more than doubled between January 2025 and June 2026 <Cite k="vago-roads" />.

Spending did rise. Pavement maintenance went from $466.5 million in 2022–23 to $611.7 million in 2024–25, with most of that jump in the last year. The department’s own business case says holding pavement condition where it is would cost more than $3 billion a year. So the criticism isn’t that budgets kept falling. It’s that what was spent wasn’t enough, and wasn’t always spent well.

Carroll’s government added $352 million on 30 August, which by the Auditor-General’s arithmetic takes the 2026–27 road asset management allocation to about $1.39 billion <Cite k="premier-roads-352" /> <Tag kind="funded" />. That came after the audit’s fieldwork had closed. The offsets include pushing three level-crossing removals back a year and savings claimed from the Suburban Rail Loop review. I want to see which roads improve and whether the repairs last, rather than a bigger pothole tally.

<h2 id="road-deaths" tabindex="-1">Road deaths and the pothole question</h2>

One correction to my own instincts. I’ve caught myself connecting every road tragedy to a pothole. TAC counted 295 deaths in 2023, 284 in 2024 and 291 in 2025 <Cite k="tac-annual" />. Those numbers are terrible, and rural roads carry more than their share, but they don’t show deaths climbing exponentially and they don’t identify potholes as the cause. My concern about safety is real; the causal claim needs evidence I don’t have.
