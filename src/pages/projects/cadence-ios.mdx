## Leaving the laptop behind without losing the workflow

Building [Cadence for Mac](/projects/cadence) solved the desk problem: you draft a script in a clean Notes-style library—or shape it from Cursor, Claude, or Gemini over Cadence's local MCP server—and read it right underneath the MacBook camera notch.

The catch shows up the moment you want to film standing up, outdoors, or with a better camera sensor than a laptop webcam. Most iPhone teleprompter apps force you to copy scripts across manually, charge a subscription for 4K or voice tracking, treat the camera like a basic preview box, or turn bright outdoor scenes into unreadable white text over a white sky.

Cadence iOS is the native iPhone companion built inside the same Swift 6 repository as Cadence for Mac. It shares the exact same script parser, phonetic aligner, scroll physics, local speech engines, and two-way library sync (`CadenceCore`, `CadenceSpeech`, and `CadenceSync`), then wraps them around a full-bleed `AVCaptureSession` camera studio designed for filming on a phone.

## Keeping your eyes on the iPhone lens

Eye drift is even more obvious on an iPhone than on a laptop because the camera lens sits close to your face. Spread text across the full width of the display and your eyes visibly scan left to right on video.

By default, Cadence iOS constrains the teleprompter card (`PrompterOverlayCard`) to a narrower 78% column centred directly beneath the Dynamic Island or notch in portrait, with the active reading anchor tucked near the top at 14% height. Rotate to landscape and the card automatically flips to hug whichever side holds the camera lens, while the shutter button, camera flip, and controls drawer move into a vertical Pro Rail on the opposite side so your hand never blocks your sightline.

Once you hit record, the studio switches into what I've called **Pure Lens** mode. The card footer dissolves into a 3-point hairline progress bar, the utility strip hides, and a subtle crimson tally border glows around the screen edge so you can tell you're rolling without staring at UI chrome. If you stumble over a line mid-take, you don't have to stop the camera: swipe right across the script to jump cleanly back to the start of the previous sentence or `# Section` cue, and Cadence drops a `Retake` chapter marker into the recording automatically.

## Solving the two hardest mobile teleprompter bugs

Two engineering problems make or break an iPhone voice-following teleprompter in practice, and neither is obvious until you build one.

The first is **audio session contention**. On iOS, running `AVCaptureSession` to record video and microphone audio at the same time as a separate `AVAudioEngine` tap for speech recognition tends to fight over hardware routing, drop frames, or stall transcription. Cadence iOS avoids that completely with `SharedCaptureAudioFeed`: a single `AVCaptureAudioDataOutput` feeds both `AVAssetWriter` (on its own serial writer queue) and a lock-free-friendly PCM bridge that normalises hardware samples into mono Float32 buffers for whichever on-device speech engine you pick. The same feed drives the 5-band Goertzel volume beam and clipping peak LED without blocking the main thread.

On iOS, **NVIDIA Parakeet Realtime EOU** (~160 ms chunks on the Apple Neural Engine) is the featured voice-following engine, with Apple's built-in zero-download `SpeechAnalyzer` active immediately as a fallback while optional models download—and both Moonshine v2 Tiny and Small available in Studio Settings.

The second problem is **frame jank and text legibility** over live video. Pushing 60–120 Hz scroll updates through SwiftUI view invalidations burns battery and competes with 4K video encoding. Instead, `PrompterScrollBridge` pushes scroll offsets and active-word indices straight into a UIKit TextKit 1 canvas driven by `CADisplayLink`—and puts the display link completely to sleep at 0 Hz the moment you pause and the scroll settles.

For legibility against changing backgrounds, `SmartContrastPixelSampler` reads a 16×8 luminance grid straight from the camera `CVPixelBuffer`'s Y-plane (handling both 8-bit SDR and 10-bit HLG HDR with zero GPU or Metal allocations) inside the exact region behind the text card. That reading feeds `SmartContrastStateMachine`, which uses dual-threshold hysteresis (`0.62` to switch to dark charcoal ink, `0.44` to return to light ink) and two-sample confirmation so rapid head movement never causes text to flicker. On busy or mid-grey scenes where you've dialled background opacity low, an automatic protective scrim fades in smoothly to keep words readable.

## A camera studio that respects how video gets edited

Because the whole point of filming on an iPhone is getting usable footage out of it, I didn't want the camera controls to feel tacked on. `CameraStudioEngine` uses Apple's `AVCaptureDevice.RotationCoordinator` to keep video buffers in native unrotated sensor space—avoiding CPU overhead from rotating 4K pixels—and applies orientation cleanly via `AVAssetWriterInput.transform`.

The four-tab **Creator Controls Drawer** (Prompter, Camera, Video, Audio) gives direct access to 1080p or 4K recording in `.mov` or `.mp4`, HEVC or H.264, 10-bit HDR where supported, Standard or Cinematic stabilisation, and 24, 25, 30, 50, or 60 fps—including **25 and 50 fps** specifically so indoor filming in Australia doesn't pick up 50 Hz LED flicker. You also get tap-to-focus, long-press AE/AF lock, manual exposure bias (`-2.0` to `+2.0 EV`), manual Kelvin (`2500–8500K`) and tint white balance with presets, multi-cam `0.5× / 1× / 2×` zoom pills, directional cardioid mic beamforming matched to the active lens, and volume-button or Bluetooth shutter support.

Small post-production details make a bigger difference than I expected. Whenever your voice crosses a `# Section` heading during a `.mov` take (or you swipe right to redo a line), Cadence embeds **QuickTime timed chapter markers** directly in the file so Final Cut Pro, DaVinci Resolve, and Premiere show your script sections on the timeline. Finished takes save straight into a dedicated **Cadence** album in Apple Photos—with crash-safe `PendingTakes` recovery if the app is interrupted—followed by a compact review bar with one-tap AirDrop/Share and **Delete & Retake** from the top or the current cue.

## Two-way script sync with Cadence for Mac

The iOS workspace mirrors the Mac app's Apple Notes-style layout: folders, pinned scripts, search, `.md`/`.txt`/`.rtf` import, and a cursor-aware keyboard bar for inserting `# Section`, `[pause]`, `[smile]`, `[breathe]`, and `**bold**` cues.

Syncing between Mac and iPhone uses the shared `CadenceSync` module introduced in [Cadence 1.2.0](/projects/cadence). Both transports run the same deterministic Last-Write-Wins merger (`LibrarySyncMerger`) with 30-day permanent-delete tombstones while keeping device-local prompter settings separate:

- **Local Wi-Fi Sync (Bonjour):** Discovers your Mac over `_cadence-sync._tcp`, pairs once with a 2-minute 6-digit code, and syncs completely offline with no cloud account.
- **iCloud CloudKit Sync:** Uses a private `CadenceLibraryZone` with incremental `CKServerChangeToken` fetches so scripts written on your Mac—or edited by an AI tool through Cadence's MCP server—appear on your phone over Wi-Fi or cellular.

If you edit a script on your Mac while you're already standing in front of the iPhone camera, Cadence never resets your take mid-read; it surfaces a quiet **"Updated on Mac · Reload"** pill when you're ready.

## Where the iOS build is now

Cadence iOS is a working **iOS 26.0+** iPhone build (configured for TestFlight and CloudKit inside the shared `cadence` Xcode project), though it isn't on the App Store yet.

A few practical boundaries are worth stating plainly. Like the Mac release, it targets modern iOS 26 APIs (`SpeechAnalyzer` and Swift 6 concurrency) and iPhone hardware only (`TARGETED_DEVICE_FAMILY = 1`, no iPad or Mac Catalyst target). When sideloaded with a free personal Apple ID, CloudKit entitlements aren't available—so `CloudKitSyncEngine` detects that upfront and stays quietly off while local Wi-Fi Bonjour sync works offline, and finished recordings still save immediately into Apple Photos so nothing is lost if a 7-day sideload expires. On older 4–6 GB RAM iPhones, keeping 4K recording and local speech models comfortable together also meant adopting `whileInUse` model retention and releasing cached ASR models whenever the app moves to the background.

There's no App Store link to share yet. Once TestFlight testing settles the remaining device, thermal, and signing details across a wider range of iPhones, I plan to ship it alongside the Mac app.
