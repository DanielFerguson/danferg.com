# Federal Labor government: research archive

**Research cut-off: 13 September 2026.**

**Scope:** the Albanese government's first term, from May 2022 to May 2025, and its ongoing second term, from May 2025 to the research cut-off.

**Status:** working evidence archive. This is neither an exhaustive inventory nor a publication-ready verdict on the federal government.

This archive preserves the federal research behind a request for a succinct, sourced account of Labor's accomplishments, accompanied by substantiated criticism. It records implemented changes, the evidence for them, attribution limits and matters that need further investigation. It does not assign a personal political position to the author.

## Reading the evidence

- **Operating:** an entitlement, institution, service or legal requirement is in force.
- **Reported delivery:** a source records activity or outputs, with the measurement period retained.
- **Legislated future delivery:** legislation exists, but the relevant future services, places or outcomes have not yet been delivered.
- **Independent adverse finding:** a court, auditor or watchdog reached a stated conclusion. Keep the conclusion's legal and factual limits.
- **Allegation or open question:** a claim has not been established by the evidence retained here.

Government departments are useful primary sources for legislation, eligibility, commencement dates and administrative counts. Ministerial statements establish what ministers announced or reported; they are not independent proof of program effectiveness. Regulators, auditors and parliamentary research add different forms of scrutiny. The source label and the caveat matter as much as the headline.

Credit should distinguish government policy and funding from decisions made by the independent Fair Work Commission, delivery by states and providers, inherited programs, parliamentary negotiation and broader economic conditions. A law being enacted is an accomplishment in implementation; it does not by itself prove the intended social outcome has occurred.

The second term is still in progress. Figures dated 2025 or earlier remain historical measurements even where the linked page is still live.

## Achievements and reforms supported by the retained evidence

### F01. Lower PBS co-payments and longer prescriptions

**Status:** operating across both terms.

- From 1 January 2023, the maximum general PBS co-payment fell from $42.50 to $30.
- From 1 January 2026, it fell from $31.60 to $25. The concessional co-payment remains $7.70 until 2030.
- Sixty-day prescriptions were introduced in three stages from September 2023 to September 2024, covering almost 300 medicines at the completion of that rollout.

**Limits:** co-payments are maximum patient contributions, not the price of every medicine. Brand premiums may apply. Longer prescriptions depend on the medicine and clinical suitability. Do not turn maximum or modelled savings into savings experienced by every patient.

**Sources:** [Health Department's retrospective confirmation of the 2023 reduction](https://www.health.gov.au/news/save-more-on-pharmaceutical-benefits-scheme-pbs-medicines); [current cheaper-medicines information](https://www.health.gov.au/cheaper-medicines); [60-day prescription patient FAQ, December 2024](https://www.health.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-12/60-day-prescriptions-patient-frequently-asked-questions.pdf).

### F02. Medicare Urgent Care Clinics and broader bulk-billing incentives

**Status:** operating; clinic rollout reported complete.

All 137 promised Medicare Urgent Care Clinics were operating by 17 June 2026. These clinics provide bulk-billed urgent care for conditions that do not require an emergency department.

From 1 November 2025, bulk-billing incentives extended to all Medicare-eligible patients. The Bulk Billing Practice Incentive Program adds a 12.5% payment on eligible Medicare benefits for participating practices that bulk bill all eligible services, divided between the practice and GP.

**Limits:** the incentive expansion does not make every GP consultation free. The government's aspiration for nine in ten GP visits to be bulk billed by 2030 is a future target, not an achieved result. Opening clinics is different from establishing the size of any reduction in emergency-department demand.

**Sources:** [Prime Minister's 17 June 2026 completion announcement](https://www.pm.gov.au/media/albanese-government-delivers-all-medicare-urgent-care-clinics-promised); [Health Department's operating bulk-billing program](https://www.health.gov.au/our-work/bulk-billing-incentives-in-general-practice/about); [commencement and patient-benefit factsheet](https://www.health.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-10/bulk-billing-incentives-in-general-practice-patient-benefits.pdf).

### F03. Higher childcare subsidies and a three-day subsidy minimum

**Status:** operating across both terms.

The maximum standard Child Care Subsidy rose from 85% to 90% from 10 July 2023, alongside changes increasing assistance across the income scale. From 5 January 2026, all CCS-eligible families can access at least 72 subsidised hours per fortnight, regardless of work or study activity.

**Limits:** the three-day guarantee concerns subsidised hours. It guarantees neither a place at a provider nor care without a gap fee. The 90% figure is the maximum standard subsidy, subject to income and hourly fee caps. Provider fees and availability affect the practical benefit.

**Sources:** [Parliamentary Library explanation of the July 2023 changes](https://www.aph.gov.au/About_Parliament/Parliamentary_departments/Parliamentary_Library/Research/FlagPost/2023/June/July_2023_Child_Care_Subsidy_changes); [Education Department's current three-day guarantee rules](https://www.education.gov.au/early-childhood/providers/child-care-subsidy/3-day-guarantee).

### F04. Expanded paid parental leave and superannuation

**Status:** operating; final scheduled leave expansion commenced July 2026.

Eligible families with a child born or adopted from 1 July 2026 can share 130 days, equivalent to 26 working weeks, of government Parental Leave Pay. A 12% superannuation contribution applies to the payment for eligible children born or adopted from 1 July 2025, with the first contributions paid from July 2026.

**Attribution and limits:** Labor expanded an existing national scheme. This is not 26 weeks for each parent. Eligibility, reserved partner days and the timing of super payments matter.

**Source:** [Services Australia: scheme changes, updated 1 July 2026](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/paid-parental-leave-scheme-changes?context=64479).

### F05. Student-debt reduction, indexation changes and repayment relief

**Status:** implemented across both terms.

The Education Department confirms that the ATO completed the one-off 20% reduction of eligible HELP and other student-loan debt. It removed more than $16 billion for over three million borrowers, applying to balances effective from 1 June 2025.

HELP indexation now uses the lower of CPI and the Wage Price Index, with retrospective credits for 2023 and 2024. The 2025–26 minimum repayment threshold rose to $67,000, alongside a system calculating repayments on income above specified thresholds rather than the previous whole-income approach.

**Limits:** the $67,000 threshold is for 2025–26; do not present it as a permanently fixed threshold. A debt reduction is not a cash payment and does not automatically reduce everyone's compulsory repayment immediately. Indexation protection concerns the published wage index, not an individual borrower's pay increase.

**Sources:** [Education Department: completed debt and repayment changes](https://www.education.gov.au/higher-education-loan-program/help-students/help-indexation-and-debt-reduction); [31 July 2025 passage and effective-date explanation](https://www.education.gov.au/higher-education-loan-program/announcements/legislation-reduce-student-loan-debt-20-passes-parliament).

### F06. Fee-Free TAFE delivered at scale

**Status:** operating with reported enrolments and completions; continuing places legislated.

The latest retained departmental snapshot, published 9 September 2026, records more than 851,000 enrolments and 276,990 completions from 1 January 2023 to 30 June 2026. Leading courses include individual support, early-childhood education and care, and nursing.

The Free TAFE Act 2025 underpins at least 100,000 fee-free places annually from 2027.

**Attribution and limits:** this is a federal–state partnership. Enrolments are not unique students, and enrolments are not completions. Future annual places remain future delivery. State implementation differs; Victoria already had a Free TAFE program before Albanese took office.

**Sources:** [June 2026 program snapshot, published September 2026](https://www.dewr.gov.au/download/17801/fee-free-tafe-program-snapshot-quarter-2-2026/43727/fee-free-tafe-program-snapshot-quarter-2-2026/pdf); [DEWR program and continuing legislation](https://www.dewr.gov.au/skills-reform/free-tafe).

### F07. Commonwealth Prac Payment

**Status:** operating from 1 July 2025.

Eligible teaching, nursing, midwifery and social-work students can receive support during mandatory placements. The payment is $338.60 per week in 2026, with support also available to eligible Diploma of Nursing students.

**Limits:** eligibility and means testing apply; this is placement support rather than a wage. The extension to ten allied-health disciplines, announced in July 2026, starts in July 2027 and is not yet delivered.

**Sources:** [Education Department's current Commonwealth Prac Payment information](https://www.education.gov.au/commonwealth-prac-payment-cpp); [VET payment rates and reporting](https://www.yourcareer.gov.au/commonwealth-prac-payment-vet/additional-information/faq).

### F08. Funded pay increases for aged-care and early-childhood workers

**Status:** operating across both terms.

The Commonwealth funded Fair Work Commission award increases beginning with a 15% rise for many aged-care direct-care workers in 2023. Further increases followed during 2025–26; the department reports approximately 400,000 aged-care workers benefited across the completed work-value case.

The early-childhood worker-retention payment funds a 15% wage increase for eligible workers at participating services, phased in from December 2024 and fully reached in December 2025.

**Attribution and limits:** the independent FWC determined aged-care award increases; government supported and funded them. The early-childhood grant has eligibility and provider conditions. Do not describe either program as a uniform increase for every worker in the broader care economy.

**Sources:** [Health Department: aged-care wage decisions and funding](https://www.health.gov.au/topics/aged-care-workforce/what-were-doing/better-and-fairer-wages?language=en); [Education Department: worker-retention payment and milestones](https://www.education.gov.au/early-childhood/providers/workforce/worker-retention-payment).

### F09. Aged-care staffing standards and a new rights-based Act

**Status:** legal requirements operating; compliance and service outcomes require separate assessment.

The 24/7 on-site registered-nurse requirement commenced in July 2023. Mandatory direct-care targets increased from October 2024 to a sector average of 215 minutes per resident daily, including 44 registered-nurse minutes.

The Aged Care Act 2024 commenced on 1 November 2025, alongside Support at Home. It introduced a Statement of Rights, supported decision-making arrangements and stronger whistleblower protections.

**Attribution and limits:** the Royal Commission and reform groundwork span governments. Labor accelerated 24/7 nursing and increased care-minute requirements. Some homes have exemptions; a legal requirement does not establish universal compliance. Commencement of the Act does not establish that access, affordability or quality problems have been resolved.

**Sources:** [Aged Care Quality and Safety Commission: staffing changes](https://www.agedcarequality.gov.au/resource-library/247-registered-nurse-coverage-and-care-minutes-residential-aged-care); [My Aged Care: care minutes and exemptions](https://www.myagedcare.gov.au/nursing-and-personal-care-aged-care-homes); [new Act commencement](https://www.health.gov.au/our-work/aged-care-act); [rights and protections](https://www.health.gov.au/our-work/aged-care-act/about); [ANAO's earlier assessment and reform chronology](https://www.anao.gov.au/work/performance-audit/design-and-early-implementation-residential-aged-care-reforms).

### F10. Paid domestic-violence leave, a right to disconnect and wage-theft penalties

**Status:** operating.

- Ten days' paid family and domestic violence leave, including for casual employees, was fully in place by August 2023.
- The right to refuse unreasonable work contact outside working hours began in August 2024 and extended to small-business employees in August 2025.
- Federal criminal penalties for intentional wage underpayment began on 1 January 2025.

**Limits:** the right to disconnect is subject to a reasonableness test. The criminal offence concerns intentional underpayment, not honest mistakes. A legal protection and effective enforcement are different questions.

**Sources:** [Fair Work Ombudsman: paid domestic-violence leave commencement](https://library.fairwork.gov.au/viewer/?krn=K700232); [right to disconnect](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/about-us/workplace-laws/legislation-changes/closing-loopholes/right-to-disconnect); [criminal underpayment laws in operation](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/newsroom/news/new-criminal-underpayment-laws-have-started).

### F11. Labour-hire pay protection and gig-worker standards

**Status:** enabling laws and specified protections operating.

Protected-pay orders for labour-hire workers have operated from November 2024. Where an FWC order applies, covered workers must receive at least the rate they would receive under the host's relevant enterprise agreement or other applicable instrument.

Eligible platform workers can challenge unfair deactivation occurring from February 2025. The first enforceable minimum-pay-and-conditions order for on-demand delivery workers commenced on 17 August 2026.

**Attribution and limits:** government enacted the framework; the independent FWC makes the orders. Labour-hire protection is subject to an order and exceptions, not automatic parity for all contractors. The August 2026 order covers specified delivery work; it does not establish a universal wage floor for every kind of gig work. Deactivation eligibility includes a minimum period of regular work and an earnings threshold.

**Sources:** [Fair Work Ombudsman: labour-hire changes](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/about-us/workplace-laws/legislation-changes/closing-loopholes/labour-hire-changes); [FWC: unfair-deactivation protection](https://www.fwc.gov.au/job-loss-or-dismissal/unfair-deactivation-or-termination-regulated-workers/unfair-deactivation); [Fair Work Ombudsman: first delivery-worker order](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/about-us/workplace-laws/fair-work-commission-orders/minimum-standards-order-on-demand-delivery-workers).

### F12. Respect@Work and employer gender-pay-gap transparency

**Status:** operating.

The positive duty requiring employers to take reasonable and proportionate measures to prevent workplace sexual harassment and related discrimination commenced on 12 December 2022. AHRC enforcement powers commenced in December 2023.

Employer-level gender pay gaps for private-sector employers with at least 100 employees were first publicly released on 27 February 2024, following legislation expanding WGEA's publication powers.

**Attribution and limits:** Respect@Work builds on the AHRC inquiry and earlier legislative changes. Employer-level disclosure expanded existing reporting; Labor did not invent gender-pay-gap measurement. A gender pay gap is not automatically evidence of unlawful unequal pay for identical work. The reforms provide mechanisms for prevention and accountability, not proof discrimination has ended.

**Sources:** [AHRC: December 2022 changes](https://humanrights.gov.au/resource-hub/by-resource-type/publications/sex-and-gender-rights/fact-sheet-respectwork-changes-sex-discrimination-act-1984-ahrc-act-1986-december-2022); [WGEA: first employer-level publication](https://www.wgea.gov.au/newsroom/employer-gender-pay-gaps-published-first-time-Media-release).

### F13. Redesigned income-tax cuts and a further 2026 rate reduction

**Status:** operating.

Labor redesigned the inherited Stage 3 tax package for commencement in July 2024, including relief for people earning below $45,000 and larger cuts for many middle earners than the original design provided. The lowest marginal rate then fell from 16% to 15% on 1 July 2026.

**Attribution and limits:** the original Stage 3 package was Coalition legislation; Labor's contribution was its redesign and subsequent cuts. The further reduction to 14% starts in July 2027. Benefits depend on taxable income; tax cuts do not assist people with no income-tax liability in the same way.

**Sources:** [Parliamentary Library: the 2024 redesign](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Bills_Legislation/bd/bd2324a/24bd042a); [enacted tax-rate schedule effective July 2026](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A03348/2026-07-01/2026-07-01/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html).

### F14. Increased income support and longer single-parent payment eligibility

**Status:** operating.

Working-age and student-payment base rates increased by $40 per fortnight in September 2023. Maximum Commonwealth Rent Assistance increased by 15% in 2023 and a further 10% in 2024. From September 2023, Parenting Payment Single eligibility extended until the youngest child turns 14, up from eight.

**Limits:** these discretionary changes are separate from routine indexation. The two rent-assistance increases should not be casually described as a simple 25% cumulative increase. Eligibility, household circumstances and rent paid determine the amount received. These changes do not demonstrate that payments are adequate against poverty or housing-cost benchmarks.

**Sources:** [DSS: payment and 2023 rent-assistance increases](https://www.dss.gov.au/jobseeker-student-payments-and-commonwealth-rent-assistance); [DSS: additional 2024 rent-assistance increase](https://www.dss.gov.au/system/files/resources/2405-004-budget-factsheet-social-security-v13-web.pdf); [Services Australia 2023–24 annual report: implemented parenting-payment change](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-10/annual-report-2023-24.pdf).

### F15. Climate legislation, industrial emissions limits and vehicle standards

**Status:** legislation and policy frameworks operating; emissions targets remain future outcomes.

In 2022 the government legislated a 43% emissions-reduction target for 2030, against 2005 levels, and net zero by 2050. Safeguard Mechanism reforms commenced in July 2023, placing major industrial facilities on declining emissions baselines. The New Vehicle Efficiency Standard commenced in January 2025.

**Attribution and limits:** the Safeguard Mechanism existed before Labor; the government strengthened it. The existence of a target or baseline does not prove aggregate emissions targets will be met. The vehicle standard is a supply-side regulation; promised lifetime fuel savings are not automatically realised savings for current drivers.

**Sources:** [DCCEEW: legislated climate framework](https://www.dcceew.gov.au/energy/strategies-and-frameworks/powering-australia); [Safeguard Mechanism overview and inheritance](https://www.dcceew.gov.au/climate-change/emissions-reporting/national-greenhouse-energy-reporting-scheme/safeguard-mechanism/overview); [Infrastructure Department: New Vehicle Efficiency Standard](https://www.infrastructure.gov.au/infrastructure-transport-vehicles/vehicles/new-vehicle-efficiency-standard/australias-new-vehicle-efficiency-standard-frequently-asked-questions).

### F16. Home-battery installation support

**Status:** operating from July 2025, with substantial reported uptake.

The Cheaper Home Batteries Program provides approximately 30% support toward the upfront installation cost of eligible systems. The Clean Energy Regulator reported more than 400,000 installed systems by mid-May 2026, including validated and pending applications, with 11.4 GWh of usable capacity.

**Limits:** subsidy settings changed in May 2026 and taper by system size. The cited count has an explicit validation caveat and measurement date. Not every installation can be assumed to have occurred solely because of the subsidy. Benefits differ for households without suitable housing, existing solar or the funds for the remaining cost.

**Sources:** [DCCEEW: current program settings](https://www.dcceew.gov.au/energy/programs/cheaper-home-batteries); [CER: June 2026 release reporting mid-May installations](https://cer.gov.au/news-and-media/media/2026/june/record-battery-and-solar-growth-reshaping-energy-grid); [CER: later June-quarter analysis](https://cer.gov.au/markets/reports-and-data/quarterly-carbon-market-reports/quarterly-carbon-market-report-june-quarter-2026/consumer-energy-resources).

### F17. National Anti-Corruption Commission

**Status:** operating from 1 July 2023.

The government established the NACC with broad jurisdiction over serious or systemic corruption involving Commonwealth public officials.

**Limits:** establishing an integrity institution is distinct from demonstrating its effectiveness. Public hearings have statutory restrictions, and the commission's own conduct has attracted substantiated criticism; see A02 and A03 below. A list of published investigations is not a comprehensive register of all misconduct and cannot establish that all current ministers are free of wrongdoing.

**Sources:** [NACC commencement statement](https://www.nacc.gov.au/news-and-media/historic-moment-national-anti-corruption-commission-starts-work); [NACC overview and statutory framework](https://www.nacc.gov.au/about-nacc/overview).

### F18. Administrative Review Tribunal and appointment safeguards

**Status:** operating from 14 October 2024.

The Administrative Review Tribunal replaced the AAT. Its legislation requires publicly advertised, merit-based suitability assessment for appointments and explicitly includes independent review among the tribunal's objectives.

**Limits:** these are institutional and appointment reforms. They do not prove political influence or backlogs have been eliminated. Any claim about improved decision quality or timeliness needs a separate assessment of outcomes, including the tribunal's workload.

**Sources:** [ART president's inaugural explanation of the statutory safeguards](https://www.art.gov.au/help-and-resources/information-researchers/speeches-and-papers/presidents-speech-inaugural-ceremonial-sitting-administrative-review-tribunal); [Attorney-General's Department: current appointment process](https://www.ag.gov.au/about-us/careers/statutory-appointments/appointments-administrative-review-tribunal); [ART 2024–25 annual report](https://www.art.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-10/Administrative%20Review%20Tribunal%20Annual%20Report%202024-25.pdf).

### F19. Restored export access to China

**Status:** trade impediments removed by December 2024.

DFAT records that dialogue and WTO advocacy helped secure China's agreement to remove the trade impediments imposed on about $20 billion of Australian exports. The final restrictions affecting red-meat establishments and live rock lobster were lifted in December 2024.

**Attribution and limits:** the result involved government diplomacy, officials, industry and China's decision to lift its restrictions. It is not equivalent to $20 billion in additional annual exports caused by Labor. Keep this separate from tariff reductions scheduled under the earlier China–Australia Free Trade Agreement.

**Sources:** [DFAT's Australia in the World 2025 snapshot](https://www.dfat.gov.au/publications/international-relations/australia-world-2025-snapshot); [20 December 2024 confirmation of live-rock-lobster trade reopening](https://www.trademinister.gov.au/minister/don-farrell/media-release/effective-immediately-live-rock-lobster-exports-china-set-restart); [DFAT 2024–25 annual report, trade-impediment result](https://www.dfat.gov.au/sites/default/files/dfat-annual-report-2024-25.pdf).

### F20. Two recorded budget surpluses

**Status:** completed financial outcomes, followed by a deficit.

The general-government sector recorded underlying cash surpluses of $22.1 billion in 2022–23 and $15.8 billion in 2023–24. The 2024–25 Final Budget Outcome then recorded an underlying cash deficit of just under $10 billion.

**Attribution and limits:** these are outcomes under Labor, influenced by commodity prices, the labour market, tax receipts and spending decisions. They cannot be attributed wholly to government policy. Keep underlying cash, headline cash and accrual balances distinct. Do not describe the budget as remaining in surplus or present a surplus alone as a complete measure of economic wellbeing.

**Sources:** [2023–24 Final Budget Outcome, confirming both surpluses](https://archive.budget.gov.au/2023-24/fbo/download/01_part_1.pdf); [AP's contemporaneous account of the first surplus and its drivers](https://apnews.com/article/60ebd8582ce75349d1c1baf7242a4251); [2024–25 Final Budget Outcome](https://archive.budget.gov.au/2024-25/fbo/download/01_part_1.pdf).

### F21. Housing investment: established funding, incomplete construction

**Status:** program established; substantial delivery remains ahead.

The $10 billion Housing Australia Future Fund and National Housing Accord Facility had supported 1,432 completed homes by May 2026, against a combined 40,000-home target by June 2029. See A01 for the independent delivery assessment.

**Limit:** neither committed funding nor contracted dwellings should be described as completed homes.

**Source:** [ANAO's July 2026 HAFF and NHAF audit](https://www.anao.gov.au/work/performance-audit/design-and-delivery-of-the-haff-and-the-nhaf).

### F22. National EPA and staged environment-law reform

**Status:** legislation passed and the new regulator established; broader implementation remains in progress.

Parliament passed the Environment Protection Reform Bills on 28 November 2025. The first provisions commenced on Royal Assent, 1 December 2025, and the National Environmental Protection Agency was established on 1 July 2026.

Four National Environmental Standards were made during the August 2026 implementation stage. Their application to projects begins with the next assessment-and-approval stage, scheduled to commence on or before 1 December 2026. Supporting regulations remained under consultation at the research cut-off.

**Limits:** these dates establish legislative and institutional delivery. They do not establish full implementation of the reform package, improved biodiversity outcomes, more effective enforcement or faster assessments. Preserve each provision's commencement and application date when assessing delivery.

**Sources:** [DCCEEW: environment protection reforms, updated 9 September 2026](https://www.dcceew.gov.au/environment/epbc/epbc-act-reform); [DCCEEW: implementation stages and standards](https://www.dcceew.gov.au/environment/epbc/epbc-act-reform/progress-on-implementation).

## Adverse findings and the limits of opposition allegations

This section includes substantiated criticism and findings that limit a stronger allegation. A performance audit, statutory misconduct finding, criminal conviction and political accusation are different categories. The fact that criticism concerns an institution created during Labor's term does not make it a personal finding against the Prime Minister.

### A01. HAFF and NHAF: independently established delivery-governance weaknesses

**Finding:** ANAO, 21 July 2026; performance and administration, not corruption.

Treasury's design was assessed as largely effective, but delivery arrangements were partly effective. Core oversight was established late; performance reporting did not give a sufficiently clear picture. The June 2029 completion forecast carried considerable uncertainty. Treasury agreed to five recommendations.

The audit also records average government support of $770,387 per dwelling in nominal terms over 25 years ($392,518 in present-value terms). This is not an average construction price.

**Opposition connection:** Shadow Housing Minister Andrew Bragg asked the ANAO to expand the audit in February 2026; the request and response are public.

**Sources:** [Full audit and recommendations](https://www.anao.gov.au/work/performance-audit/design-and-delivery-of-the-haff-and-the-nhaf); [Bragg's request and Auditor-General's response](https://www.anao.gov.au/work/request/expansion-of-the-anao-audit-the-housing-australia-future-fund-haff).

### A02. NACC handling of Robodebt referrals: officer misconduct finding

**Finding:** NACC Inspector, 30 October 2024; statutory officer misconduct.

The Inspector found that Commissioner Paul Brereton had not adequately removed himself from decision-making after declaring a conflict concerning a referred person. Delegating the formal decision was insufficient given his continued involvement. The report also found the NACC's public explanation misleading about the APSC's ability to sanction referred people.

**Limits:** the Inspector classified the conduct as officer misconduct arising from a mistake of law or fact, expressly describing it as not unlawful. The assessment concerned apprehended, not established actual, partiality. It was not a finding of corrupt conduct by Albanese or a Labor minister.

**Source:** [Inspector's October 2024 investigation report, especially executive summary](https://www.naccinspector.gov.au/system/files/2024-10/The-Inspectors-Investigation-Report.pdf).

### A03. Later NACC Inspector inquiries: discontinued proceedings are not findings

**Status update:** July 2026.

The shared research records Brereton's resignation taking effect on 6 July 2026 and two other unfinished Inspector inquiries being discontinued. Those discontinued inquiries do not supply final misconduct or corruption findings. Keep them separate from the completed October 2024 finding.

**Source:** [Inspector's July 2026 statement under section 230(2)](https://www.naccinspector.gov.au/system/files/2026-07/inspectors-statement-under-s-230-2-nacc-act.pdf).

**Retrieval note:** this update and exact URL were verified in the coordinating research. Reopening the PDF while assembling this file returned a non-retryable web-tool safe-open error. Refresh the source before using this later development in published prose; do not enlarge the conclusion beyond the recorded procedural status.

### A04. Mobile Black Spot Program: genuine prioritisation questions, narrower audit conclusion

**Finding:** ANAO, 22 May 2024; largely effective administration with identified weaknesses.

Forty of 54 target locations (74%) were in Labor-held electorates. The department's advice did not adequately compare those locations' relative merits with other mobile black spots. The audit nevertheless found design and funding largely effective and the minister's funding decisions consistent with the Commonwealth grants framework.

**Opposition connection:** Shadow Communications Minister David Coleman requested an audit in March and April 2023, alleging political allocation and misuse.

**Limit:** the electorate distribution and weak comparative needs advice support scrutiny of prioritisation. This audit does not substantiate a claim of proven illegal or corrupt pork-barrelling. Conversely, procedural compliance does not settle every question about the fairness of the original target selection.

**Sources:** [ANAO audit](https://www.anao.gov.au/work/performance-audit/award-funding-under-the-mobile-black-spot-program); [Coleman's letters and Auditor-General's response](https://www.anao.gov.au/work/request/round-6-the-mobile-black-spot-program).

### A05. Conclusions this research does not support

- That all opposition criticisms are either proven or fabricated.
- That all Labor ministers have been cleared of corruption because a public NACC listing does not identify a finding against them.
- That an investigation, resignation or referral establishes guilt.
- That a procedural or performance failure necessarily meets the statutory definition of corruption.
- That a government's creation of a watchdog cancels out failings exposed in that watchdog.
- That government spending, targets, contracts, enrolments or legislative passage are interchangeable with completed services or measured benefits.
- That favourable national economic indicators are caused solely by the government in office.

These are methodological limits, not separate adverse findings.

## What would need more research before a federal article

1. **Medicare outcomes:** compare consistent bulk-billing measures and patient out-of-pocket costs over time; assess urgent-care evaluation results and geographic access. Avoid mixing service-level and patient-level rates.
2. **Aged-care outcomes:** check actual compliance with staffing standards, the operation of exemptions, waiting times, Support at Home costs and the implementation of rights and complaints mechanisms.
3. **Childcare affordability:** distinguish the subsidy change from subsequent provider-fee increases, availability and the effect on different household incomes.
4. **Education outcomes:** investigate course completion and employment outcomes without treating students still enrolled as failures. Evaluate eligibility and delivery experience for prac payments.
5. **Workplace enforcement:** investigate actual protected-pay orders, wage-theft enforcement, the delivery-worker order and practical use of the right to disconnect. Separate statute from demonstrated enforcement.
6. **Housing delivery:** refresh completions and construction status, audit-recommendation implementation, delivery forecasts and the financing model. Compare alternatives on a consistent basis.
7. **Climate and energy:** assess emissions trajectories, the use of offsets, grid constraints, renewable capacity, battery-policy distribution and realised household costs. Do not infer causation from rollout totals alone.
