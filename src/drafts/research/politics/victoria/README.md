# Victorian election guide: evidence ledger

Status: working research, not a published article. Last checked: **13 September 2026** (Australia/Melbourne). Updates are manual; this file does not create a monitoring task.

Purpose: support an evolving Victorian election guide that starts with the government's record, tests regional concerns, and later compares the alternatives. Daniel's experience and judgement belong in the article; this ledger records what the evidence can establish. It is a selected record, not an exhaustive inventory or an endorsement score.

## Evidence conventions

- Give both the event/data period and the source's publication/update date where available. An undated page is marked as checked, rather than assigned an invented publication date.
- Separate operating services, completed infrastructure, enacted laws, announced funding, future targets, reported outcomes and author calculations.
- Government releases establish the government's actions and claims; they do not independently prove effectiveness, value for money or causation. Prefer regulators, auditors, legislation and administrative data where those answer the question.
- Credit originating and completing governments, responsible ministers, public servants and other partners where material. Federal and state funding can overlap.
- Distinguish a substantiated administrative finding, an integrity finding, a criminal charge, a conviction, an unresolved allegation and political criticism. Use the actual finding and its limits.
- A search that has not located a policy means "not yet verified", not "the party has no policy".

## Selected accomplishments: delivered status and attribution

### VIC-A01 — Metro Tunnel

**Operating.** Five underground stations and twin nine-kilometre tunnels opened on 30 November 2025. The full Cranbourne/Pakenham/Sunbury line switch occurred on 1 February 2026. Andrews commenced delivery; Allan opened it. Opening is established; benefits and value for money require separate assessment.

Source: [Big Build project overview and milestone history](https://bigbuild.vic.gov.au/projects/metro-tunnel/about/overview), checked 13 September 2026.

### VIC-A02 — Level crossing removals

**94 crossings removed**, with the latest milestone and new Melton station opening on 1 September 2026. This is cumulative Andrews–Allan–Carroll delivery. Some Melton station precinct works continue into 2027. The program's 110-crossing total is a future target, currently listed for 2033.

Sources: [Melton opening and 94-crossing milestone](https://bigbuild.vic.gov.au/news/level-crossing-removal-project/new-melton-station-opens-with-94-level-crossings-gone-for-good), 1 September 2026; [program status](https://bigbuild.vic.gov.au/projects/level-crossing-removal-project), checked 13 September 2026.

### VIC-A03 — West Gate Tunnel

**Operating from 14 December 2025.** Delivered twin tunnels, freeway widening and another river crossing. Andrews initiated; Allan completed. Do not turn forecast traffic or travel-time benefits into measured results, or treat completion as evidence of good value.

Source: [Big Build completed project](https://bigbuild.vic.gov.au/projects/west-gate-tunnel-project), checked 13 September 2026.

### VIC-A04 — Public transport affordability

**Implemented.** Regional fares were capped at metropolitan rates on 31 March 2023 under Andrews. From 1 January 2026, under-18s with a Youth myki received free statewide travel; eligible seniors and concession groups received free weekend travel under Allan. The original $9.20 regional cap is a historical amount, not the current fare.

Sources: [Public Transport Ombudsman: regional fare cap](https://www.ptovic.com.au/news-publications/news/the-daily-travel-cap-in-regional-victoria-now-matches-fares-in-metro-melbourne), 2023 account checked 13 September 2026; [Transport Victoria: free travel](https://transport.vic.gov.au/news-and-resources/news/free-travel-for-children-and-eligible-concession-travellers), published 2 December 2025.

### VIC-A05 — New schools

**Opened.** VSBA reports 121 new schools since 2017, including 19 in 2026. Attribute the continuing construction program across Andrews and Allan, including Carroll's work as education minister; do not attribute all 121 to Carroll's premiership.

Sources: [VSBA account](https://schoolbuildings.vic.gov.au/about-us) and [2026 school openings](https://www.schoolbuildings.vic.gov.au/back-to-school-2026), checked 13 September 2026.

### VIC-A06 — Free TAFE

**Operating since 2019.** In April 2026 the government reported more than 245,000 students benefiting and over $816 million in waived tuition. These are government-reported participation/savings figures, not completion or employment outcomes. Victoria's scheme predates Albanese's national program; subsequent funding overlaps.

Sources: [Government's Free TAFE account](https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/free-tafe-bigger-better-guaranteed), 24 April 2026; [Victorian Skills Plan 2025 into 2026](https://www.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-01/victorian-skills-plan-for-2025-into-2026.pdf), January 2026 publication confirming the 2019 start.

### VIC-A07 — Free Kinder

**Operating at participating services statewide since 2023.** Covers funded three- and four-year-old programs. Andrews introduced it; Allan continued expansion. Free Kinder does not remove all long-day-care charges, and universal 15-hour three-year-old provision remains a rollout rather than completed delivery.

Sources: [Current Free Kinder rules](https://www.vic.gov.au/free-kinder) and [commencement history](https://www.vic.gov.au/when-start-kindergarten), updated 26 March 2026.

### VIC-A08 — Hospitals

**Opened to patients.** The Victorian Heart Hospital opened in February 2023 under Andrews. The replacement Footscray Hospital opened on 18 February 2026 under Allan following Andrews-era planning and construction. An opened replacement is not a net increase equal to its entire bed capacity; check staffed capacity and service outcomes separately.

Sources: [Victorian Heart Hospital](https://www.vhba.vic.gov.au/health/specialist-centres/victorian-heart-hospital), checked 13 September 2026; [Footscray opening](https://www.vhba.vic.gov.au/news/new-footscray-hospital-opens-its-doors-patients), updated 18 February 2026.

### VIC-A08b — Bendigo Hospital and shared credit

Opened January 2017; second stage completed June 2018. Builder appointed May 2013 and construction began June 2014, before Andrews took office. Completion under Labor deserves shared credit with the predecessor government, Bendigo Health and delivery partners.

Source: [VHBA project history](https://www.vhba.vic.gov.au/health/hospitals/bendigo-hospital), checked 13 September 2026. Distinguish the January 2017 patient opening, June 2018 completion and August 2018 ceremonial opening.

### VIC-A09 — Nurse and midwife ratios

**Legislated protection in 2015.** The Safe Patient Care Act establishes staffing requirements in specified publicly funded services, with later improvements. Ratios already existed in Victorian industrial agreements from 2000; Andrews protected them in statute rather than originating ratios altogether.

Source: [Health Department legislative history](https://www.health.vic.gov.au/nursing-and-midwifery/nursing-and-midwifery-legislation-and-regulation), checked 13 September 2026.

### VIC-A10 — Family violence response

**Services and institutional reforms delivered.** Specialist courts, information sharing and the statewide Orange Door network form part of the response. On 28 January 2023, the Andrews government reported implementing all 227 Royal Commission recommendations. This is an implementation claim, not proof that every reform achieved its purpose or that violence declined.

Source: [Implementation announcement](https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/landmark-royal-commission-recommendations-implemented), 28 January 2023. Source trap: a generic government progress page incorrectly describes the Royal Commission as completed in 2015; it was established in 2015 and reported in 2016.

### VIC-A11 — Renters' rights

**In force.** Earlier minimum standards and pet protections were followed by the 25 November 2025 changes: ending no-fault evictions including at fixed-term expiry, banning acceptance of above-advertised rent, and longer notice periods. Application/privacy and third-party fee changes commenced 31 March 2026. These protect legal rights; rental affordability and enforcement outcomes need independent assessment. Energy-efficiency requirements beginning in 2027 are future implementation.

Source: [Consumer Affairs Victoria commencement table](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/new-changes-to-the-rental-laws), updated 30 March 2026.

### VIC-A12 — Renewable electricity and household solar

**Reported generation and installed systems.** Renewables supplied 42.4% of Victorian electricity generation in 2024–25. Solar Victoria reported more than 350,000 rooftop systems installed through its support by May 2026. State policy, federal schemes, private investment and household spending share credit. Electricity generation is not all energy consumption; installations are not unique households or approvals.

Sources: [Official renewable-energy progress](https://www.energy.vic.gov.au/renewable-energy/victorian-renewable-energy-and-storage-targets), 2024–25 reporting period, checked 13 September 2026; [Solar Victoria milestone](https://www.solar.vic.gov.au/solar-victoria-hits-500000-approvals), 28 May 2026. The milestone headline counts approvals; the claim here uses its installed-system account.

### VIC-A13 — Voluntary assisted dying

**Access commenced 19 June 2019**, following the 2017 Act under Andrews. Credit the government that established the scheme while recognising the conscience vote and support across party lines. This is a legal and service reform; people's moral assessment can differ.

Source: [Review Board report of operations 2019–20](https://www.health.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-07/voluntary-assisted-dying-review-board-report-operations-2019-2020.pdf), covering the first operating year.

### VIC-A14 — Conversion-practice prohibition

**In force from 17 February 2022.** The 2021 Act under Andrews established a civil reporting, education and investigation framework, with criminal offences in specified circumstances.

Source: [Victorian Equal Opportunity and Human Rights Commission](https://www.humanrights.vic.gov.au/change-or-suppression-practices/reporting-practices/), checked 13 September 2026.

### VIC-A15 — Statewide Treaty

**Commenced 12 December 2025**, after Royal Assent on 13 November. Andrews laid foundations; Allan concluded the agreement with the First Peoples' Assembly, which deserves explicit shared credit. Permanent representation, truth telling and accountability are institutional achievements; better long-term social outcomes remain to be demonstrated.

Source: [Joint government–Assembly negotiation and commencement statements](https://www.firstpeoplesrelations.vic.gov.au/treaty-negotiation-updates), dated statements through 12 December 2025, checked 13 September 2026.

### VIC-A16 — Public fertility care

**Operating since 2022.** Includes fertility treatment and the public egg and sperm bank established in July 2023. The Health Department reported 250 babies born through the program in its milestone account; treat that as the source's reported milestone, not a September 2026 total.

Sources: [Program](https://www.health.vic.gov.au/public-health/public-fertility-care), checked 13 September 2026; [250-baby milestone](https://www.health.vic.gov.au/news/public-fertility-care-welcomes-250th-baby), page updated 3 February 2026.

### Accomplishments still requiring separate verification

Do not count Suburban Rail Loop, airport rail, all 110 crossings, universal 30-hour Pre-Prep or all promised Big Housing Build dwellings as completed. Reconcile new social housing with demolitions, replacements, net stock and actual occupancy before adding a quantified housing-delivery claim. Mental health, regional health access, public housing and environmental outcomes warrant their own records rather than being inferred from spending announcements.

## Regional roads: deterioration, funding and safety

### VIC-R01 — Independent condition and maintenance evidence

Source: [VAGO, Maintaining State Roads](https://www.audit.vic.gov.au/report/maintaining-state-roads), tabled **9 September 2026**, especially sections 3–5 and the linked dashboard/raw datasets.

- Remaining useful life: 48% of state roads were poor/very poor in 2024, versus 39% in 2021; regional figure 50.38%.
- Poor means 5–15 years remaining; very poor under five. Neither classification necessarily means immediately unsafe.
- The recorded-versus-repaired defect gap more than doubled, January 2025–June 2026. Records can contain multiple defects.
- DTP warned of insufficient funding and reactive maintenance. VAGO identified weak reporting, prioritisation documentation and contractor oversight.
- Pavement spending: $466.5m in 2022–23; $611.7m in 2024–25. Earlier comparable expenditure was unavailable.
- Maintaining condition while replacing expired assets: over $3bn annually in DTP's estimate, not VAGO's independent costing.
- The 2026 survey was unvalidated, expected October. Scope: state roads, not council roads.
- DTP reported $1.39bn funding after August's addition, which was outside the audit's scope.

Interpretation to retain: the evidence supports deteriorating condition and a growing maintenance problem. It does not establish exponential pothole growth, crash causation, or a uniform metro/regional pattern.

### VIC-R02 — Funding and the Carroll response

**Budget commitment:** the May 2026 budget described over $1bn for road maintenance. Keep the scope of each budget measure clear. [Budget account](https://www.budget.vic.gov.au/making-life-easier), updated 2 June 2026.

**Announced 30 August 2026:** Carroll added $352m and targeted 500,000 annual pothole repairs, versus 250,000. He identified a one-year delay for three level-crossing removals and other proposed savings as funding sources. Treat the repair target as prospective and the crossing delay as a timing change, not independently verified lifetime savings. [Government announcement](https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/safer-roads-and-fixing-potholes-our-priority).

**Operational baseline:** DTP says crews have repaired an average of 250,000 potholes annually since 2019. This is a repair count, not a count of newly formed or outstanding potholes. [Maintenance explanation](https://transport.vic.gov.au/road-and-active-transport/business-and-industry/road-and-traffic-management/road-maintenance/more-information-on-road-maintenance), checked 13 September 2026.

Next assessment: distinguish budget from outturn, all road assets from pavement, patching from rehabilitation, and nominal spending from real purchasing power. Select relevant local routes, identify their owner and examine condition and completed work. Do not infer that a particular neglected road was caused by a particular Melbourne project's budget.

### VIC-R03 — Road deaths and the limits of crash data

TAC's annual totals are 295 deaths in 2023, 284 in 2024 and 291 in 2025. Rural Victoria recorded 148 in 2024 and 157 in 2025. These fluctuate rather than establish exponential growth. [TAC annual figures](https://www.tac.vic.gov.au/road-safety/statistics/lives-lost-annual), calendar years, checked 13 September 2026.

The current comparable-period snapshot runs to **midnight 10 September 2026**: 175 deaths statewide versus 205 in 2025; rural Victoria 97 versus 108. These are provisional deaths, not all crashes, and are subject to revision. [TAC year-to-date figures](https://www.tac.vic.gov.au/road-safety/statistics/lives-lost-year-to-date).

The public fatal/injury crash dataset was updated 10 August 2026 and covers events through 31 December 2025, with a stated reporting lag. The road-surface-condition field records dry/wet/muddy/snowy/icy conditions, not pothole condition or cause. Establishing pothole-related injury would require more specific evidence; proximity alone would not prove causation. [Dataset and scope](https://opendata.transport.vic.gov.au/dataset/victoria-road-crash-data?v=1), [surface-condition dictionary](https://opendata.transport.vic.gov.au/dataset/victoria-road-crash-data/resource/17965f05-2dda-4351-b32c-67038c74b6b3).

## Emergency Services and Volunteers Fund

### VIC-E01 — What is in force

ESVF replaced FSPL on **1 July 2025**. Councils collect it through rates notices. It supports fire services plus a wider set of emergency response and recovery agencies. Liability is a fixed charge plus the applicable variable rate multiplied by capital improved value (CIV), less applicable relief. CIV includes land, buildings and improvements; the levy is not based on current income or farm profit. Classification follows land use, not a blanket country-versus-Melbourne division.

Sources: [SRO fund explanation](https://sro.vic.gov.au/owning-property/emergency-services-and-volunteers-fund/understanding-emergency-services-and-volunteers-fund), updated 22 May 2026; [calculation and classification rules](https://www.sro.vic.gov.au/owning-property/emergency-services-and-volunteers-fund/emergency-services-and-volunteers-fund-calculations-and-discounts), updated 24 August 2026.

Sources: [SRO current rates](https://www.sro.vic.gov.au/about-us/rates-and-statistics/current-rates/emergency-services-and-volunteers-fund-current-rates), updated 21 July 2026; [SRO historical rates](https://www.sro.vic.gov.au/about-us/rates-and-statistics/historical-rates/emergency-services-and-volunteers-fund-and-fire-services-property-levy-historical-rates), updated 7 August 2026.

**Author calculation: constant $1,000,000 CIV, one assessment, no concessions or rebates. These are illustrative properties, not Daniel's assessments or representative averages.**

- Residence, 2024–25: $132 + ($1,000,000 × 0.000087) = **$219**.
- Residence, 2026–27: $139 + ($1,000,000 × 0.000173) = **$312**, an increase of $93 or **42.5%**.
- Farm, 2024–25: $267 + ($1,000,000 × 0.000287) = **$554**.
- Farm, 2026–27: $282 + ($1,000,000 × 0.000287) = **$569**, an increase of $15 or **2.7%**.
- In this same-value 2026–27 comparison, the farm pays **82.4% more** than the residence. A regional residence with the same classification, CIV and relief as a Melbourne residence has the same calculation.

The claim "country people now pay 100–200% more" is not established as a general fact. It could confuse proposed variable-rate increases, actual total bills, unequal property values or different classifications. Preserve the question about productive assets, income volatility and volunteer contributions, while testing the numbers and the chosen comparator.

### VIC-E04 — Current volunteer and farm relief

Source: [Service Victoria Volunteers Rebate Guidelines](https://service.vic.gov.au/find-services/housing-and-property/eligible-volunteers-rebate-scheme/guideline), current 2026–27 rules checked 13 September 2026; applications opened 15 July 2026.

- Eligible organisations: CFA, VICSES and Shepparton Search and Rescue Squad. Ordinary eligibility requires current qualifying volunteer status, at least 12 months' service and meeting criteria on 31 May before the levy year. Status, disciplinary suspension and leave rules apply.
- Organisational life membership can qualify; brigade/unit life membership alone does not.
- An eligible principal residence receives its levy rebate. Eligible farmland/single-farm-enterprise rebates are capped at **$1,710 for 2025–26** and **$3,152 for 2026–27**, the latter equivalent to the current levy on $10m CIV.
- Ownership requirements differ between a residence and farmland, which can include qualifying company or trust interests. One rebate per person/property per year; annual reapplication is required.
- Apply **before or after paying** the rates notice. Earlier pay-first reporting is superseded. The 2026–27 deadline is 30 June 2028.
- General community fundraising does not itself establish rebate eligibility. Check the actual volunteer and property circumstances before applying the rules to Daniel.

Separately, a qualifying single farm enterprise can avoid repeated fixed charges across multiple properties; council determines that exemption. The $50 eligible pensioner/veteran concession also continues. [SRO discounts](https://www.sro.vic.gov.au/owning-property/emergency-services-and-volunteers-fund/emergency-services-and-volunteers-fund-calculations-and-discounts), updated 24 August 2026.

### VIC-E05 — Fairness and funding questions still open

1. Compare actual bills and rebate access for representative farms and country/metro residences, including the effect of CIV movements and a possible future end to the freeze.
2. Reconcile levy revenue with total agency funding and actual capability delivered. Earmarking revenue does not by itself establish how much spending is additional to the previous budget baseline.
3. Examine fleet age, facilities, response capacity, volunteer time and continuing fundraising. Keep those resource questions separate from whether a particular volunteer qualifies for tax relief.

## Principal Victorian integrity evidence

### I1 — Operation Watts (20 July 2022)

IBAC/Ombudsman substantiated misuse of publicly funded staff for Labor factional activities, nepotism, bullying and attempted interference with grants. Adverse findings concern Adem Somyurek and Marlene Kairouz. A criminal offence was **not established** under the applicable framework. No evidence community grants paid for ALP memberships. IBAC found improper adviser pressure to award/manage a **$1.2m** Health Workers Union training-entity contract without competitive tender and despite lack of relevant experience. Breaches of public duties and ethics; no corrupt-conduct finding. Do not call the extra **$2.2m election commitment** additional stolen or necessarily spent money. Andrews's accountability for his staff is not a finding he personally received a corrupt benefit.

### I4 — Commonwealth Games withdrawal (20 March 2024 VAGO audit)

VAGO calculated **over $589m** spent securing/planning/withdrawing, including $380m settlement. Business case understated costs and overstated benefits; frank, full and timely advice was deficient. The public cost estimate exceeding $6bn was overstated and not transparent. This is audited financial/administrative failure, **not a corruption finding**.

### M1 — ACMA publication dates and separate survey series

- The *News media in Australia: 2025* written report was first released in **March 2025**. Its main web page is marked updated 12 May 2026; that is not its original publication date. The ACMA series page specifically says the first report was released March 2025 and the next is due 2027.

- Date clarification: https://www.acma.gov.au/news-media-australia
- An August 31, 2026 segmentation report adds that news habits are often driven by convenience and time; younger/time-constrained people encounter news incidentally through social media. Do not turn this into a statement that young people are uniquely gullible.
- Source: https://www.acma.gov.au/articles/2026-08/acma-report-reveals-australians-changing-news-habits

### M2 — University of Canberra Digital News Report Australia 2026

- Released **16 June 2026**, produced with Reuters Institute, University of Oxford.
- Social media overtook direct news website visits as the **primary pathway to online news**. This is different from saying most people get all their news from social media.
- 56% use social media for news, television 57%, news websites/apps 52%; 43% consume creator/influencer news.
- 21% trust social-media news, 19% chatbot news, 54% the news they personally use. Trust is a self-reported attitude, not an independent accuracy score.
- The UC and ACMA figures use different studies/timeframes. Do not join ACMA's 43% to UC's 56% as a demonstrated one-year rise.
- Source: https://www.canberra.edu.au/about-uc/media/newsroom/2026/june/dnr-2026-younger-australians-are-rewriting-the-rules-of-news-consumption
- Main findings: https://www.canberra.edu.au/research/centres/nmrc/digital-news-report-australia

### M3 — Actual 2025 election misinformation research, published August 2026

- Kruger, Howe and Saletta, University of Melbourne; peer-reviewed in HKS Misinformation Review **27 August 2026**.
- 90 active volunteers submitted 592 **potentially** misleading posts and more than 3,000 assessments during six weeks around the 2025 federal election.
- The paper documents false claims and out-of-context material; it is a study of a monitoring method, **not a representative prevalence estimate**.
- The authors expressly acknowledge the active cohort skewed left of centre and their feeds and selection may introduce ideological filtering. The statement that Labor was the most common identifiable target (53%) is therefore inappropriate as evidence that 53% of Australia's political misinformation targets Labor.
- Independent true/false validation involved only 30 posts; crowd-assessor agreement was 81%, Cohen's kappa 0.42. Do not call every one of the 592 posts independently verified false.
- The follow-up intervention was nonrandomised: better later discernment was associated with participation; causal improvement is not established.
- Source: https://misinforeview.hks.harvard.edu/article/monitoring-misinformation-building-discernment-a-crowdsourcing-study-during-the-2025-australian-federal-election/

### M4 — Bots, influence and attribution

- EIAT's **January 2025** threat overview warns about inauthentic influence and generative AI false narratives/deepfakes. It expressly says **most disinformation does not involve a foreign power**.
- Source: https://www.aec.gov.au/About_AEC/files/eiat/election-security-environment-overview.pdf
- ACMA's September 2025 fourth platform-monitoring report, printed pp.23–24, reports global network takedowns but says signatories **did not report Australia as a target of coordinated influence operations in the 2024 reporting period**. This is a reporting limit, not proof none occurred or a statement about 2026.
- Australian fake-account enforcement totals include spam, scams and fraud, are not comparable like for like and are not a political-bot count. Do not use global takedown totals to estimate Australian voters, anti-Labor commenters, or election effects.
- Source: https://www.acma.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-09/Digital%20platforms%20efforts%20to%20combat%20disinformation%20and%20misinformation_4th%20report%20to%20government_0.pdf
