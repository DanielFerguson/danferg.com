# Victorian crossbench: roads and emergency-services levy

Research cutoff and every source check: **13 September 2026 (Australia/Melbourne)**. Researcher: victorian_integrity subagent. First article remains frozen; no repository edits or outreach. This is a bounded primary-source ledger, not a complete list of every candidate or all their policies.

## Editorial conclusion

The Greens have a published country-roads objective and a current position favouring government capacity to deliver preventive maintenance. They supported the amended ESVF after negotiating changes in 2025. One Nation's current Victorian material establishes opposition to ESVF and regional-road ambitions, but the reviewed sources do not supply a comparable statewide maintenance budget, delivery schedule or quantified replacement emergency-services funding plan. Named independents must remain separate: a verified regional candidacy is not evidence of a detailed roads/levy policy, and a parliamentary vote is not a fully funded election programme.

Use **“not located in the sources reviewed as at 13 September”**, or **“this source does not specify”** for evidence gaps. Do not write “has no policy” or “unpublished” unless the party itself establishes that status. VEC nominations open 4 November and close noon 9 November: present current campaign endorsements as announced candidacies, not a final ballot. ## Comparison records

### Greens — regional roads

- **Mechanism:** current state Transport and Freight Policy calls for repairing country roads; evidence-based speed limits; regional level-crossing safety; shifting freight from road to rail; freight charges related to axle load and damage per kilometre rather than fixed registration charges. These are policy objectives, not an implementation-ready funding package. The policy also favours public transport and active transport, so it is inaccurate to describe their transport position simply as opposition to roads.
- **Current intervention:** Sarah Mansfield's 24 August 2026 release argues that government should retain capacity to deliver preventive maintenance, and opposes the sale of SprayLine's spray-sealing and line-marking operations. It predicts higher costs and weaker competition. Those predicted effects are the party's argument, not proven outcomes of the sale. The statement does **not** pledge a specified buyback, staff establishment or costed restoration programme.
- **Cost/funding/timing:** neither source specifies a statewide road-maintenance appropriation, annual resurfacing target, implementation dates or a quantified funding offset. The axle/damage charging aim has no rate or revenue estimate in the policy; do not treat it as a funded road-repair package or assume it can be implemented without national coordination.
- **Regional effects:** intended safer country roads and reduced heavy-road freight burden. Distributional consequences for freight operators and farming supply chains are not quantified. Effects of a public delivery capability depend on design, procurement, workforce and price evidence.
- **Dates:** transport policy last amended **14 June 2025**; road-maintenance release **24 August 2026**.
- **Sources:** G1, G2 below.

Suggested concise wording: “The Greens explicitly support repairing country roads and argue that government should retain its own preventive-maintenance capability. Their published policies also seek more freight on rail. I have not located a comparable maintenance budget and delivery timetable in the sources reviewed.”

### Greens — ESVF and emergency services

- **Action:** all four Greens Council MPs voted for the bill's **second reading on 15 May 2025**; the vote was 22–18. The three Assembly Greens supported the Council's suggested amendments later that day. This is an established legislative record, not a new 2026 governing offer.
- **Negotiated position:** the party's 15 May statement lists an ongoing **$10 million annually for FRV fleet replacement**, requirements concerning the levy contribution to agency budgets, restrictions on uses of levy funds, drought relief, a reduced primary-production levy rate and an additional **$15 million for farmers' energy-bill support**. Attribute these as the deal the party announced; do not describe every item as independently measured delivery.
- **Critical precision:** current Act version 035, section 12(2B)–(2C), fixes the **levy-funded shares of annual funding requirements at 90% for FRV and 95% for CFA and VicSES**. These are fixed percentages, not “at least” percentages, a 90/95% increase or a protected dollar budget. During the committee debate the Treasurer agreed that the legislation concerns the contribution to budgets, not the size of the budgets. Use the Act's fixed shares rather than adopting the party release's minimum-share wording. A headline “guaranteed funding” without the dollar-budget qualification can mislead.
- **Mechanism/funding:** retain the property levy with negotiated safeguards and relief; rates are set separately by the Treasurer, not fixed in that bill. The historical 2025 deal cannot establish which exact property rates the Greens would seek after the 2026 election. Current bill amounts/eligibility must come from SRO rather than the old party release.
- **Regional effects:** levy retained; drought and primary-producer relief intended to reduce the burden; investment supports response capability. Public-revenue replacement is not part of an abolition plan here, because their documented position was support for the amended levy.
- **Sources:** G3, P1, P2, P3.

Suggested concise wording: “The Greens supported the amended levy after negotiating fleet funding, farmer relief and restrictions on how the money could be used. That is a record of changing and supporting the scheme. It does not tell me which levy rates they would seek next term, or guarantee that each agency's overall budget can never be cut.”

These are candidate aspirations within the party, not separate costed statewide packages. Do not imply these people stand on the same ballot.

Broad promises to reduce waste or taxes do not quantify money available for roads.

### One Nation Victoria — ESVF and emergency services

- **Current affiliation:** Parliament's live profile identifies **Rikkie-Lee Tyrrell, Northern Victoria Council region, One Nation Victoria**. The official 2026 candidate list also names her for Northern Victoria. Search snippets may retain the old registered party name; the current page has the new one.
- **Current position:** a **6 May 2026** party release explicitly says the party opposes ESVF. Current **Wendouree candidate Stuart Robinson** is described by the party as leading the local campaign to scrap it. The same May release contains promises to repeal land-acquisition laws: **do not transfer “immediate repeal” timing from those separate laws to ESVF**.
- **Bill action:** Tyrrell voted against the Council second reading on 15 May 2025 and for referral of the bill to the Economy and Infrastructure Committee. That establishes opposition and support for scrutiny at that stage.
- **Emergency-service provision:** a **12 January 2026** party statement called for immediate resources and transparency on ESVF collection/allocation. It does not quantify additional funding, explain replacement revenue or give a fleet plan. Its allegations about under-resourcing and money raised are party assertions, not independently adopted facts in this ledger.
- **Cost/funding/timing/regional effects:** repealing or reducing the levy would change property charges according to the eventual design, but the reviewed sources do not establish whether the old FSPL would be restored, exactly which liabilities would disappear, how much revenue would be replaced, which general-budget offsets would pay, or when a change would take effect. Do not assert that levy abolition necessarily entails cuts; equally do not assume funding is replaced without a proposal.
- **Sources:** O2–O6, P1.

Suggested concise wording: “One Nation opposes the ESVF, and its Northern Victoria MP voted against the bill. Its current Wendouree candidate also campaigns to scrap the tax. The sources I reviewed do not specify a replacement levy design or quantified funding to maintain emergency services.”
