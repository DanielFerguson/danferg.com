# Victorian election 2026: Liberal and National roads and emergency-services evidence

This ledger records public commitments, their mechanisms and their limits. An announcement is evidence of a promise, not delivery or an independently demonstrated outcome. Primary party releases establish what the parties say; PBO, VAGO, CFA and SRO documents are used to test the underlying comparisons. Sources labelled indexed were readable through the search index but did not also load successfully as live pages. No messages were sent to third parties.

## Claims to qualify in the article

1. **The Coalition has two distinct current road commitments.** Keep the state and council packages separate. Do not portray all $5.288 billion as additional spending or assume both packages share the same starting year.
2. **The 25% road-funding claim is a party comparison awaiting reconciliation.** C14's later government addition matters. A four-year promise needs a four-year, consistently defined comparator before naming a funding winner.
3. **Repealing the ESVF does not mean abolishing every property fire levy.** Future rates, agency funding and existing volunteer rebates remain material unanswered details.
4. **A policy page's different wording is an ambiguity, not proof of disagreement.** Attribute joint releases jointly; distinguish a National MP's local advocacy, a named joint-government promise, and a minority report recommendation.
5. **The PBO regional figures need their denominator.** They measure project lifetime investment and leave a substantial mixed/unallocated category. They do not show that regional Victoria gets 12% of every annual government budget.
6. **CFA funding has more than one period and measure.** The earlier nominal grant decline is supported; the later actual result increased. Funding adequacy and inflation require further analysis beyond either headline.
7. **Savings are not interchangeable.** Ten-year attrition savings, project cancellation estimates and four-year appropriations need timing, transition costs and consistent accounting. No public independent costing found is not no costing ever done.

## Suggested article wording

The Coalition's roads offer has two parts: $5 billion for state-road maintenance over four years, and a separate $288 million programme giving 48 rural and regional councils $1.5 million a year from 2027–28. The council funding is concrete enough to assess, and letting councils choose their priorities matters. I still want to see how the broader maintenance promise is funded and what improvement we would be able to measure beyond the number of potholes repaired. [State roads](https://vic.nationals.org.au/media-releases/danny-obrien-mp-5-billion-to-eliminate-1-million-potholes-and-get-victorias-roads-back-on-track/), [council programme](https://vic.nationals.org.au/media-releases/danny-obrien-mp-liberals-and-nationals-to-restore-288-million-country-roads-and-bridges-program/), [start date](https://vic.nationals.org.au/media-releases/danny-obrien-mp-the-nationals-to-restore-country-roads-and-bridges-program/).

On emergency services, the promise is to scrap the ESVF and bring back the Fire Services Property Levy. That leaves some practical questions: what would the rates be, how would the CFA and SES be funded, and what would happen to the rebates volunteers can claim now? I haven't found a published replacement model detailed enough to answer those questions. A pledge to back volunteers needs that detail if we are going to compare what people would pay with what their brigades would receive. [Original joint commitment](https://vic.liberal.org.au/news/2025-05-20-battin-obrien-liberals-and-nationals-to-scrap), [July reaffirmation](https://vic.nationals.org.au/media-releases/danny-obrien-mp-bushfire-inquiry-confirms-labor-left-cfa-exposed-and-ill-equipped/).
