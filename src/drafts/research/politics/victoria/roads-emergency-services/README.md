## Key claim and calculation checks

| Article claim | Evidence and precise reading |
| --- | --- |
| 50.38% regional roads in poor/very poor structural condition | Government R1: [VAGO, 9 September 2026](https://www.audit.vic.gov.au/report/maintaining-state-roads). The survey year is **2024**; remaining useful life measures structural condition, not immediate road safety or pothole-caused crashes. |
| Current road-asset funding approximately $1.39 billion | Government R1–R2: $1.041bn budget + $0.352bn later addition = **$1.393bn**, rounded. Broader than pavement maintenance; the late announcement is outside the completed audit’s findings. |
| Coalition $5bn over four years | Coalition C01/C13: **total promised funding**, not $5bn on top of existing funding. $5bn ÷ 4 = $1.25bn as an arithmetic average; not a published annual appropriation or a directly comparable annual advantage over Labor. |
| Additional $288m council programme | Coalition C02–C03: **48 × $1.5m × 4 = $288m**, equivalent to $72m annually, starting 2027–28. Count the joint promise once. An equal grant does not reflect equal maintenance needs. |
| 25% Fair Share and PBO comparison | Coalition C04/C15: the guarantee concerns **new infrastructure expenditure**; PBO’s denominator is **lifetime estimated investment in listed projects**. The $100m project threshold and exclusion of identified Commonwealth contributions belong with the narrower “more than double” comparison. Other/private contributions remain. |
| $1m residential property: $312 | Government L1: **$139 + ($1,000,000 × 0.000173) = $312** for 2026–27, before applicable relief. Illustrative constant CIV, not Dan’s bill or a typical household. |
| $1m primary-production property: $569 | Government L1: **$282 + ($1,000,000 × 0.000287) = $569**. Classification, ownership, valuations and farm-enterprise arrangements matter. A frozen variable rate is not a freeze on every bill. |
| $3,152 farm volunteer cap | Government L4: [current guidelines](https://service.vic.gov.au/find-services/housing-and-property/eligible-volunteers-rebate-scheme/guideline), section 3.3. **$282 + ($10m × 0.000287) = $3,152**. The guidelines independently confirm the cap; the arithmetic alone does not establish eligibility. |
| $1.6bn ESVF receipts | Government F1: Budget Paper 5, printed p.177. **Gross 2026–27 forecast revenue**, not actual receipts or entirely additional services. |
| 90% FRV / 95% CFA and VICSES | Crossbench P3: [current Act, version 035](https://content.legislation.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-07/12-58aa035-authorised.pdf), s12(2B)–(2C), printed p.23. Fixed levy-funded shares of annual funding requirements; **not “at least”** and not a guaranteed dollar budget. |
| Capacity commitments | Government C1–C3 and Coalition C07/C11–C12. May budget figures are commitment totals with annual profiles unverified. July fleet schedule and September Hazelwood North station are actual opposition promises; the minority report’s additional recommendations remain separately attributed. |

Corroborates Coalition C03.

## Retrieval and attribution notes

The first-100-days release visibly dates itself **20 August 2026**, despite a URL containing `2026-08-19`. The live page, East Gippsland Council response and Service Victoria rebate guidance were verified in a browser when the research reader failed. Budget Paper 5 was inspected visually in the official PDF. Use displayed publication dates and reporting periods, not search-engine relative timestamps.

For the Ilias Bafas lead, a final browser visit to the supplied campaign domain on 13 September redirected to unrelated banking content. No campaign page or policy PDF could be inspected there. This is an access problem, not evidence that a policy does not exist or that a candidate has withdrawn. He is not included as a verified policy alternative. The wider independent search is recorded in `crossbench.md`.

Party pages establish what the party says. They do not independently establish that a rival caused a quantified loss, that a policy is affordable, or that a proposed saving is available for a different commitment. Current Nationals wording about the replacement levy is not identical to the more specific 2025 explanation of SES budget funding; the article preserves the stated model while leaving the eventual bill and agency funding unresolved.
