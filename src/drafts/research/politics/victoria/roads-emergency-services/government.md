**Check date and cut-off: 13 September 2026.** Scope: regional roads, emergency-service capacity and the Emergency Services and Volunteers Fund (ESVF). This is a working research ledger, not a party score, endorsement or completed comparative article. All sources below were checked on the cut-off date. ## How to compare the evidence

- **Operating rule:** the levy, eligibility requirement or service arrangement currently applies.
- **Budget/announcement:** money is allocated or a program is announced. This is not an expenditure outturn, delivered equipment or an independently established service improvement.
- **Delivered output:** a dated agency record confirms an opening, handover or completed activity. Credit may be shared with volunteers, councils, Commonwealth funding and donations.
- **Outcome:** road condition, response performance, staff availability or another result is measured. A count of dollars, potholes, vehicles or recruits does not establish this on its own.
- Compare annual amounts with annual amounts, and total project commitments with total project commitments. Keep capital, operating expenditure, depreciation and funding transfers distinct. No Appropriation Act has been independently reconciled to every initiative in this ledger.

## Roads

### R1. Independent baseline: deterioration and incomplete maintenance assurance

**Source:** Victorian Auditor-General's Office, *Maintaining State Roads*, tabled **9 September 2026**. [Report](https://www.audit.vic.gov.au/report/maintaining-state-roads); [PDF](https://www.audit.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-09/20260909_Maintaining-State-Roads.pdf), audit snapshot and printed pp. 6, 8–10, 12–21; PDF pages 11, 13–15, 17–26.

**Finding:** state roads rated poor/very poor on remaining useful life increased from 39% in 2021 to about 48% in **2024**; the regional 2024 share was **50.38%**. This structural measure does not mean those roads were immediately unsafe. Maintenance needs exceed repairs; DTP advised that funding was insufficient. Data, prioritisation and contractor assurance have gaps. Published targets provide little insight into condition or effectiveness. Six recommendations were accepted fully or in principle.

**Funding qualification:** VAGO records a $1.041 billion 2026–27 road-asset budget plus the later $352 million announcement, approximately $1.39 billion combined. This includes bridges, drainage, signs, signals and planning. The addition occurred after audit completion and is outside the findings. These are allocations, not expenditure outturns. The next condition survey was expected in October 2026.

### R2. Carroll government's additional repairs commitment

**Source:** Premier of Victoria, *Safer Roads And Fixing Potholes Our Priority*, **30 August 2026**. [Announcement](https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/safer-roads-and-fixing-potholes-our-priority), unpaginated.

**Commitment:** an additional $352 million, with funding said to flow immediately, to increase the year's pothole-repair target from 250,000 to 500,000. Work was expected to accelerate as rain eased and continue into January. Named routes include the Geelong Ring Road, Western, Calder and Princes highways, alongside regional roads.

**Financing claim:** government identifies $112 million in forward-estimates savings from deferring three level-crossing completions by one year, plus other announced savings. This does not by itself reconcile the entire $352 million or prove whole-of-project cost savings.

**Status/limits:** a funded announcement awaiting delivery evidence. Do not call 500,000 repairs completed, equate repairs with unique defects, or treat temporary patching as durable rehabilitation. The release's claims about opponents are partisan statements, not independently verified findings.

## Current ESVF rules and household/farm incidence

### L1. Current 2026–27 rates

**Source:** State Revenue Office, *Emergency services and volunteers fund (current rates)*, updated **21 July 2026**. [Rates](https://www.sro.vic.gov.au/about-us/rates-and-statistics/current-rates/emergency-services-and-volunteers-fund-current-rates), fixed-charge and variable-rate tables, unpaginated.

The ESVF replaced the Fire Services Property Levy on **1 July 2025**. For **2026–27**:

| Classification | Fixed charge | Multiplier applied to capital improved value (CIV) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Residential, including vacant residential | $139 | 0.000173 |
| Commercial | $282 | 0.001330 |
| Industrial | $282 | 0.001330 |
| Primary production | $282 | 0.000287 |
| Public benefit | $282 | 0.000057 |

**Units matter:** the primary-production rate is 28.7 **cents per $1,000 CIV**, not 28.7%, and not $28.70 per $1,000. CIV includes improvements, not just bare land. These are current rates; originally proposed rates are historical proposals.

### L2. The farm freeze and deferred residential change

**Source:** DTF, *2025–26 Budget Update*, **December 2025**, Appendix A, printed pp. **132–133**; PDF pages 138–139. [Budget Update](https://www.dtf.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-12/2025-26-Budget-Update.pdf).

**Implemented policy carried into current rates:** the primary-production variable rate remains 28.7 cents per $1,000 CIV. The planned residential fixed-charge restructuring was delayed to **1 July 2027**; residential charges continued at the earlier level indexed by CPI for 2026–27. The volunteer farmland rebate's CIV cap increased from $5 million to $10 million from 2026–27.

**Limits:** freezing a multiplier does not freeze individual bills where CIV or the fixed charge changes. The Budget Update's estimated revenue effects are policy costings, not cash paid to farmers. Do not represent the July 2027 residential change as already operating.

### L3. Calculation, concessions and farm aggregation

**Source:** SRO, *Emergency services and volunteers fund calculations and discounts*, updated **24 August 2026**. [Calculation rules](https://www.sro.vic.gov.au/owning-property/emergency-services-and-volunteers-fund/emergency-services-and-volunteers-fund-calculations-and-discounts), unpaginated.

Councils collect the state levy on rates notices. The calculation combines the fixed charge and CIV-based variable amount, less applicable relief. An eligible pensioner/veteran concession is $50 on the principal residence. A qualifying single farm enterprise spanning parcels may pay only one fixed charge; the council determines eligibility.

**Illustration, calculated from L1:** a single eligible $10 million CIV farm incurs $282 + $2,870 = **$3,152** before concessions/rebates. This is an illustration, not a household-specific tax assessment. Ownership, land classification and parcel arrangements matter.

### L4. Volunteer rebate is real, conditional and capped for farms

**Source:** Service Victoria, *Eligible Volunteers Rebate Scheme — Guidelines*, current online edition; publication/update day not displayed in retrieved text. [Guidelines](https://service.vic.gov.au/find-services/housing-and-property/eligible-volunteers-rebate-scheme/guideline), sections **2, 3.1–3.3 and 4**. Supporting [FAQ](https://www.service.vic.gov.au/find-services/housing-and-property/eligible-volunteers-rebate-scheme/faq).

For 2026–27, eligibility is assessed at **31 May 2026**; applications opened **15 July 2026** and close **30 June 2028**. Eligible CFA, VICSES and Shepparton Search and Rescue volunteers/life members can claim for their owned principal residence or qualifying farmland/single farm enterprise. Service-wide life membership qualifies; brigade/unit-only life membership does not automatically qualify. Ordinary volunteer eligibility includes at least 12 months' service and activity/leave conditions.

One rebate per person per levy year; annual application required. Principal-residence relief can cover the full eligible levy. Farm relief is capped at **$3,152 in 2026–27**, corresponding to $10 million CIV. It is not an exemption for all property owned by every volunteer.

**Retrieval qualification:** direct web opening failed, but the official guidelines and FAQ were available in the search index with the current 2026–27 clauses. The coordinating researcher subsequently verified the current cap and one-rebate/application rules on the live page through the interactive browser on 13 September. Refresh the full guidance before an individual application answer.

## What the levy funds, and what is actually additional

### F1. Current gross revenue forecast

**Source:** DTF, *2026–27 Budget Paper 5: Statement of Finances*, **5 May 2026**, printed **p. 177**; PDF page **183 of 278**. [Official landing page](https://www.dtf.vic.gov.au/2026-27-statement-finances); [official PDF](https://s3.ap-southeast-2.amazonaws.com/vicbudgetfiles2026.27vicbudget/2026-27+State+Budget+-+Statement+of+Finances.pdf).

**Directly verified:** expected ESVF receipts are **$1.6 billion in 2026–27**, rising to approximately **$1.8 billion annually from 2027–28**. Rates are set annually with regard to agency budgets, legislative collection limits and community factors. The 2026–27 increase relative to the Budget Update reflects forecast dwelling growth and assumes no variable-rate change.

**Limits:** this is gross forecast revenue, not actual receipts and not the amount of extra emergency-service spending created by the reform. The fund replaces earlier levy revenue and supports services that already existed. Page 177 was inspected visually in Chrome because the web reader returned an S3 retrieval error.

### F2. Hypothecation and additionality are different questions

**Sources:** Victorian Parliament, *Fire Services Property Amendment (Emergency Services and Volunteers Fund) Bill 2025*, Legislative Council committee debate, **15 May 2025**, exchanges between Jaclyn Symes, David Davis and Richard Welch; [Hansard](https://www.parliament.vic.gov.au/parliamentary-activity/hansard/hansard-details/HANSARD-974425065-30856), unpaginated web transcript. Premier, *Equipping Our Emergency Services For The Future*, **15 May 2025**; [announcement](https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/equipping-our-emergency-services-future).

The Treasurer explained that consolidated revenue continues to supplement levy funding. The announced arrangement assigns 95% of CFA and VICSES budgets and 90% of FRV's budget to the levy; other agencies do not have the same fixed levy-funded shares. The government committed to emergency-service use and annual reporting of receipts and spending.

**Percentage precision:** the amendment text reproduced in that primary Hansard fixes the levy-funded shares at 95% for CFA/VICSES and 90% for FRV. Prefer those fixed shares over the shorthand “at least”; this does not fix the agencies' overall dollar budgets. The Treasurer expressly agreed that distinction.

**Reasoned interpretation:** earmarking receipts for emergency services is compatible with replacing expenditure previously financed from broader revenue. It does not prove every extra levy dollar buys an additional service. Conversely, a change of funding source is not proof that money was unlawfully diverted or that no new investment occurred. To quantify net additionality, reconcile each agency's comparable baseline, new measures, expiring measures and outturns.

## Two concise current capacity commitments, beyond the levy mechanism

### C1. CFA fleet, facilities and volunteer support — funded commitments

**Source:** Victorian Budget ministerial release, *Labor Delivers What We Need To Keep Victorians Safe*, **5 May 2026**, **PDF p. 4**. [Release](https://www.dtf.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-05/2026-27-State-Budget-media-release-Labor-Delivers-What-We-Need-To-Keep-Victorians-Safe.pdf).

The budget announces **$100 million for CFA tankers and pumpers**, **$26.2 million** for station construction, planning, land and upgrades, and **$22.4 million** for volunteer clinicians, training and protective equipment. The station allocation includes different stages, not ten completed stations.

**Status:** a current government funding commitment. The release does not show the year's expenditure, all vehicles delivered, completed buildings or measured improvement in volunteer availability.

### C2. VICSES facilities and operating new units — funded commitments

**Source:** same **5 May 2026** budget release, **PDF p. 4**.

The government announces **$17.4 million** for Port Phillip and Corio facilities and Whitehorse land acquisition, plus **$3.6 million** to operationalise Kalkallo and Devon Meadows units.

**Status:** capital and operating commitments. Buying land, funding a building and having a staffed, trained, response-ready unit are separate milestones. No completed delivery is established for these initiatives by that announcement alone.

### C3. Volunteer grants — a specific additional program

**Source:** Premier, *Record $31 Million Boost For Emergency Services*, **2 April 2026**. [Release](https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/record-31-million-boost-emergency-services), unpaginated.

The release announced a 2026–27 VESEP round opening on **16 April 2026**, offering **$31 million**, with grants up to $250,000 and a government contribution of $2 per $1 raised locally. Government identifies **$62 million additional funding over four years** to double the program. This is a named increment distinguishable from gross levy receipts.

**Limits:** announcement of a grant round is not a completed procurement program. The funding model retains local fundraising, and grants cover multiple volunteer organisations. Check awards and handovers before counting delivery.

## Delivered agency outputs and capacity constraints

### D1. CFA: completed work, with attribution and workforce caveats

**Source:** CFA, *Annual Report 2024–25*, reporting year ended **30 June 2025**, publication day not verified. [Report](https://www.cfa.vic.gov.au/ArticleDocuments/520/2024-25-cfa-annual-report.pdf.aspx), printed **pp. 4–5, 30–31, 50, 52, 65**; corresponding PDF pages **6–7, 32–33, 52, 54, 67**.

Reported outputs include **147 emergency-response vehicles**, completion of stations at **Natte Yallock, Beaufort and Irymple**, and **9,200 portable radios** by June 2025. Five other stations were under construction. The $138 million government-funded radio replacement program concluded that year.

The operational-volunteer count was **28,753**, against a target range of **35,000–37,400**; total volunteers included support members and juniors. Do not treat the entire volunteer roll as immediately deployable firefighters. The report says **28% of the operational fleet was brigade-funded**. Government funding, brigade fundraising and service delivery deserve separate credit. These 2024–25 outputs predate the levy commencing and cannot be retroactively attributed to ESVF receipts.

### D2. VICSES: completed facilities and support, with comparability limits

**Source:** VICSES, *Victoria State Emergency Service Annual Report 2024–25*, statements authorised **23 September 2025**, reporting year ended **30 June 2025**. [Report](https://www.ses.vic.gov.au/documents/d/www/victoria-state-emergency-service-authority-annual-report-2024-25), printed **pp. 30, 40–41, 56–57, 73**; PDF pages one higher.

New units at **Aintree, Cranbourne, Point Cook and Officer** opened in December 2024. Renewal upgrades were completed at **32 units**, with others unfinished. These are delivered examples; they are not all regional locations.

At June 2025 there were **4,390 active volunteers** (4,077 operational; 313 support), plus 650 non-active/on leave. The report notes changed definitions shifting members between categories. Some fixed-term staff became permanent because of new funding; permanence is not necessarily an extra worker. Road-crash-rescue response met its benchmark in **94%** of cases, against 90%. The new rescue-vehicle build panel had been delayed. These figures are historical measurements, not a verified September 2026 staffing or fleet inventory.

### D3. A newer VICSES public snapshot, with narrower assurance

**Source:** VICSES, *About VICSES*, linking *VICSES at a glance 2025–26*, publication/update day not displayed. [Agency page](https://www.ses.vic.gov.au/-/about-vicses), unpaginated; the infographic's accessible description.

The current agency page reports **5,098 volunteers**, **338 staff**, **319,221 volunteer hours** and **32,934 incidents** for 2025–26. This is a newer agency-reported snapshot, not a verified audited annual-report workforce table. Do not directly compare its total volunteers with D2's active volunteers or infer that every listed person was available to respond. These counts do not establish the effect of the levy or the delivery status of C2.

## Important open questions

- **Roads:** which work packages and durable repair methods are funded by the latest addition; what has been spent and completed; how will condition and defect recurrence be published; what changes implement the audit recommendations; how does a multi-year prevention program compare with reactive repair targets?
- **Levy:** obtain the first annual receipt/allocation reconciliation, administrative and rebate costs, arrears and collection shortfalls; quantify agency-by-agency funding additional to the pre-reform baseline rather than treating an earmarked tax as evidence of additionality.
- **Capacity:** obtain current fleet ages, vehicle downtime, station milestones, active qualified volunteer availability, vacancies/secondee coverage and retention; verify delivery of the two 2026–27 commitments above.
- **Comparison:** establish comparable jurisdiction, hazard exposure, population/geography, service responsibilities and capital/operating definitions before making interstate funding claims. Government claims to have the best-funded services are not independently established here.
- **Pending source checks:** direct DPS totals and their full scope; newer CFA/VICSES annual reports or detailed 2025–26 outturns beyond D3's public snapshot. Basic jurisdiction is recorded above. Web-reader failures are retrieval limitations, not evidence the documents do not exist; the unverified aggregate figures in F3 should be omitted from article prose.
