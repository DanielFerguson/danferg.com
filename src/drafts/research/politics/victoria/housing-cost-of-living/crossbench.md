# Crossbench positions on housing and cost of living — Victoria 2026

Research notes for the housing/cost-of-living article. Date checked for every item: **2026-09-15**. Election: Saturday 28 November 2026.

Reading status key: **FULL** = live page fetched and read; **PARTIAL** = fetched but paywalled/truncated; **INDEX** = seen only in search results or via a secondary aggregator.

Note on this research method: pages were fetched through a summarising tool, so "full" means the page was retrieved and read, not that every line is reproduced here. Quotes under 15 words are as returned by that tool and should be spot-checked against the live page before publication.

## (a) Victorian Greens

### A1. Rent freeze and permanent rent cap
- **Title:** Fastest rent hikes in Australia show why Victoria needs rent caps – saving renters $15,590 by 2030
- **Publisher:** Australian Greens Victoria (media release, Gabrielle de Vietri, housing spokesperson)
- **Published:** 9 July 2026 | **URL:** https://greens.org.au/vic/news/media-release/fastest-rent-hikes-australia-show-why-victoria-needs-rent-caps-saving | **Status:** FULL
- **Quote:** "Renters don't need more excuses. They need lower rents."
- **Mechanism:** two-year rent freeze, then a permanent cap on increases at the lower of wage growth or CPI; cap attaches to the property, not the tenancy; a two-year freeze applies at the start of each new tenancy.
- **Funding/period:** none stated (regulatory measure). Claimed saving to a median Victorian renter of $15,590 by 2030 is attributed to Parliamentary Budget Office modelling; a companion figure of $120,406 by 2040 appears in the Election Tracker summary (INDEX, not read in the release itself). PBO document itself not located — treat the numbers as "Greens-cited PBO modelling, not independently verified".
- **Unspecified:** enforcement, treatment of new builds, regional vs metro differentiation, interaction with existing 12-month increase rule.

### A2. Public housing build, stamp duty abolition, "ultra-wealth" land tax
- **Title:** Greens unveil bold housing plan to bring down the cost of buying a home by abolishing stamp duty
- **Publisher:** Australian Greens Victoria | **Published:** 9 July 2026
- **URL:** https://greens.org.au/vic/news/media-release/greens-unveil-bold-housing-plan-bring-down-cost-buying-home-abolishing-stamp | **Status:** FULL
- **Quote:** "Stamp duty is a huge barrier for people buying their first home, moving or downsizing."
- **Mechanism:** abolish stamp duty on owner-occupied homes up to $1.1m, sliding scale to $1.6m, stated goal of full phase-out; build "more than 88,000" public homes with a $4 billion initial investment (build, maintain, upgrade).
- **Funding:** higher land tax on investors holding $5m+ in residential investment property. **Discrepancy:** the release (as read) states 5.3% rate and "$6.4 billion over ten years"; the Greens' platform page (A4) states "$2 billion over four years". Both are Greens figures; neither independently verified. Resolve before quoting either.
- **Period:** ten-year revenue horizon implied; no build timetable.
- **Unspecified:** regional allocation of the 88,000 homes; stamp-duty revenue replacement; timing of phase-out.

### A3. Affordable-housing definition
- Election Tracker (INDEX) records a Greens commitment dated 16 July 2026 to "legislate a clear, consistent definition of affordable housing linked to what households earn". The Greens release itself was not fetched. Platform page (A4) puts it as rent within 30% of tenant income.

### A4. Platform page (standing policy)
- **Title:** Make Housing Affordable | **Publisher:** Australian Greens Victoria | **Published:** undated
- **URL:** https://greens.org.au/vic/platform/housing | **Status:** FULL
- **Quote:** "fighting alongside public housing communities to save their homes while Labor tries to destroy them"
- **Content:** rent caps; 88,000 public homes / $4b; oppose demolition of the 44 public housing towers; 30% "genuinely affordable" housing required in developments of 15+ dwellings statewide; ban sale/lease of public housing land to private developers; legislated goal to end homelessness by 2036; claims credit for the short-stay levy and owners-corporation power to ban short-stays, and says it will "work with councils" on short-stays.
- **Regional Victoria:** no specific mention on this page.

### A5. Towers redevelopment opposition
- **Title:** Public housing tower demolitions and alleged misconduct towards residents referred to Ombudsman
- **Publisher:** Australian Greens Victoria | **Published:** 17 June 2026
- **URL:** https://greens.org.au/vic/news/media-release/public-housing-tower-demolitions-and-alleged-misconduct-towards-residents | **Status:** FULL
- **Quote:** "Labor is tearing down good, affordable homes and replacing them with expensive private apartments."
- **Mechanism:** referral to the Ombudsman on relocation practices and human-rights compatibility. No costed alternative (retrofit) is stated in this release.
- Context (INDEX only): ABC, 29 Jan 2026, "Public housing towers for the elderly to go in next stage of high-rise redevelopment plan", https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-01-29/seven-melbourne-high-rise-public-housing-towers-redevelopment/106283344. The towers are all in metropolitan Melbourne; relevance to a regional reader is indirect (state capital allocation).

### A6. Cost of living (Election Tracker summary, INDEX)
- Permanent free public transport "metropolitan and regional trains, trams and buses" — https://greens.org.au/vic/campaigns/make-public-transport-free (not fetched); supermarket price-gouging regulation — https://greens.org.au/vic/platform/economy (not fetched). Costings not seen.
- Aggregator: Greens policy profile, Australian Election Tracker, last updated 2 Aug 2026, https://electiontracker.au/elections/vic/2026/parties/greens/policies (FULL).

### A7. Historic Greens demands (for context only)
- "Greens issue challenge to government on housing plan" (19 Oct 2023) and "Vacancy tax bill delayed" (2 Nov 2023) set out the 2023 demands: 2-year freeze then cap, 90-day short-stay cap, 30% inclusionary zoning, 100,000 public homes in a decade, 3% vacant land tax. URLs: https://greens.org.au/vic/news/media-release/greens-issue-challenge-government-housing-plan-fix-crisis-or-we-wont-support ; https://greens.org.au/vic/news/media-release/vacancy-tax-bill-delayed-following-greens-pressure-strong-housing-reform (both FULL). Note the public-housing target moved from 100,000 (2023) to "more than 88,000" (2026).

## (b) One Nation Victoria

### B1. Housing policy page
- **Title:** Housing Policy | **Publisher:** One Nation Victoria | **Published:** undated (Election Tracker "verified 2 Aug 2026")
- **URL:** https://vic.onenation.org.au/housing-policy | **Status:** FULL
- **Quote:** "Other countries ... have acted to ban foreign ownership in recent years"
- **Mechanisms listed:** cut immigration to "sustainable levels" (immigration is federal — no state mechanism given); ban non-citizen ownership of residential property with a transition period to sell; allow superannuation to be invested in one's own home; review/cut state fees, taxes and charges (page asserts these are ~45% of a new home's cost — **not verified**); abolish stamp duty on insurance premiums; tax-free renting of rooms in a primary residence and lifting restrictions on renting granny flats to non-family (cites South Australia as model); faster approvals and land releases, release of government land; private-sector incentives and community-organisation partnerships for low-cost housing; oppose a "rent tax" payable to Aboriginal corporations (no such proposal is identified on the page).
- **Figures on page:** ~$500,000 average new-build cost; ~1% vacancy "urban and regional" — both **not verified**.
- **Funding/period:** none stated; page says more policy will come "over the course of the election cycle".
- **Regional Victoria:** referred to generically ("urban and regional"); no regional-specific measure.

## (c) Other minor parties

**Victorian Socialists** — "Housing for all", https://www.victoriansocialists.org.au/policies/housing-for-all (FULL, undated): 15,000 public homes a year for 10+ years (150,000); "a 25 percent cut to all residential tenancies followed by a five-year rent freeze", then caps at half of CPI/WPI; a State Bank of Victoria; publicly owned builder. No regional-specific measure; no costing. Candidates in Benambra, Bass, Bendigo East/West, Eureka, Geelong, Lara, Macedon, Mildura, Narracan, Ripon, South Barwon, South-West Coast, Wendouree (Wikipedia list, INDEX).

**Libertarian Party** — policy hub https://www.vic.libertarians.org.au/policies (FULL) lists "Housing Crisis", "Zoning Out" and "Tax Relief" pages but the hub itself carries no detail; sub-pages not fetched. Nothing regional found. Libertarian MLC David Limbrick opposed the short-stay levy (Hansard, D1).

**Legalise Cannabis, Animal Justice, Shooters Fishers Farmers, Family First** — no published state housing policy relevant to regional Victoria was found in this pass (searches returned only candidate lists and unrelated pages). Legalise Cannabis supported the short-stay levy (D1). Family First has candidates in Eureka, Lowan and Narracan; AJP in Benambra, Eureka, Macedon, Wendouree (Wikipedia, INDEX). Absence of a policy in these searches is a research limit, not a finding.

## (e) Crossbench votes on key 2024–26 housing bills

### D1. Short Stay Levy Bill 2024 (7.5% levy from 1 Jan 2025; 25% of revenue earmarked for regional Victoria)
- Parliament of Victoria explainer, 19 Sept 2024, https://www.parliament.vic.gov.au/news/economy/short-stay-levy/ (FULL): passed Assembly with Labor for, Coalition against.
- Legislative Council second reading, 17 Oct 2024, Hansard https://www.parliament.vic.gov.au/parliamentary-activity/hansard/hansard-details/HANSARD-974425065-28482 (FULL for debate; **division numbers not in the extract read**): Greens supported after securing owners-corporation power to ban short-stays (75% vote) and council/SRO data-sharing; Legalise Cannabis supported; Coalition and Libertarian opposed. Positions of Animal Justice, One Nation (Tyrrell) and Shooters not captured — **not verified**. Regional line from the Coalition: "This levy will hit regional Victoria the hardest" (Wendy Lovell and others, on Murray border towns).
- Owners-corporation power confirmed operating from Jan 2025: Southbank Local News, 6 Nov 2024, https://www.southbanklocalnews.com.au/power-of-the-people-short-stay-levy-bill-2024-passed-in-victoria/ (FULL).

### D2. Consumer and Planning Legislation Amendment (Housing Statement Reform) Bill 2024 (rental reforms: no-fault eviction end, rental bidding ban, etc.)
- Council debate 20 Feb 2025, https://www.parliament.vic.gov.au/parliamentary-activity/hansard/hansard-details/HANSARD-974425065-29840 (FULL for that sitting day; **final vote not in this extract**). Greens claimed the reforms as a "huge win for renters" (https://greens.org.au/vic/news/huge-win-renters-parliament-set-pass-reforms-end-no-grounds-evictions-and-rental-bidding, INDEX). Regional colour from Wendy Lovell (Lib, Northern Victoria): in Bright a "roster of which restaurants would open on which nights" because of worker housing shortage; "In Bendigo, nearly 40 per cent of rental properties sold ... went to owner-occupiers". Party-by-party third-reading record **not verified**.

### D3. Residential Tenancies Amendment (Rent Controls) Bill 2026 (Greens private member's bill)
- Legislative Assembly, 1 April 2026, https://www.parliament.vic.gov.au/parliamentary-activity/hansard/hansard-details/HANSARD-2145855009-35166 (FULL). Motion to introduce defeated 31–48: Greens (de Vietri, Sandell, Read) plus Coalition members voted to allow introduction; Labor voted no. Tim Read: "property investors are flourishing while renters struggle to make ends meet." Labor's reply framed rent control against its "150 rental reforms". Note the Coalition vote was procedural (to allow debate), not support for rent caps.

### D4. Activity-centre / townhouse-code planning changes
- These were made by ministerial planning-scheme amendment, not by bills, so there is no crossbench vote to record. Greens' standing position (A4) is 30% affordable housing in 15+ dwelling developments and community participation in planning (https://greens.org.au/vic/policies/planning-policy, INDEX). No One Nation vote or position on activity centres found. Regional relevance is limited: the activity-centre program is metropolitan.

## Gaps and research limits

1. **Division lists.** Third-reading divisions for the short-stay levy (Oct 2024) and the rental-reform omnibus (Feb/Mar 2025) in the Legislative Council were not in the Hansard pages read; Animal Justice, One Nation (Tyrrell) and Shooters votes are unrecorded here. Pull the Council Minutes of Proceedings for those dates.
2. **Greens costings.** Two different revenue figures for the same land-tax measure ($6.4b/10 yrs vs $2b/4 yrs) appear on Greens pages; the PBO documents behind the $15,590 and $4b figures were not located. Greens' 2026 free-public-transport and supermarket policies were not fetched.
3. **One Nation.** Housing page is undated and uncosted; its immigration and superannuation planks are federal. Candidate list conflicts (Shepparton, Murray Plains) between the party site, local press and Wikipedia. The cost-of-living page was read only via an aggregator.
4. **Polling.** Only the July Resolve poll was read from a primary reporting source; August–September figures come from a secondary aggregator. No published model tests a Liberal–One Nation arrangement.
5. **Independents.** Local-press articles (Border Mail, Sunraysia Daily) are paywalled; housing positions for McKeeman and Manning rest on one paragraph each. Ali Cupper's Mildura candidacy is unconfirmed. Candidate fields will change until nominations close in November; re-check Wikipedia, Tally Room and VEC then.
6. **Minor parties.** No regional housing policy was found for Legalise Cannabis, AJP, SFF or Family First; the Libertarian sub-pages were not opened. Silence here reflects search coverage, not a settled finding.
7. **Regional specificity.** No crossbench actor found had a regional-Victoria-specific housing measure beyond the government's 25% regional earmark of the short-stay levy; that absence is itself worth stating carefully in the article as "none found", not "none exists".
