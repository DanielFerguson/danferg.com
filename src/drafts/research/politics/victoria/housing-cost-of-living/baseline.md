# Baseline: housing and cost of living in Victoria (independent/official data)

Research notes for a blog article on the 28 November 2026 Victorian state election. Date checked for every source: **2026-09-15**. "Read live" means the page was fetched and read; "indexed only" means the title/snippet was seen in search results but the page was not read. Media sources are marked **media**. Figures not confirmed from a fetched page are marked **not verified**.

Status labels: **measured outcome** (independent statistical series), **independent finding** (non-government analysis), **government figure** (state-published number), **promise / funded / delivered** used where relevant.

## 1. Rents

### 1.1 Homes Victoria Rental Report (official)
- **Title:** Rental report (landing page, latest quarter shown: September quarter 2025)
- **Publisher:** Department of Families, Fairness and Housing / Homes Victoria
- **Published:** quarter to Sept 2025 (release date not shown on page)
- **URL:** https://www.dffh.vic.gov.au/publications/rental-report
- **Read:** live, landing page only (Word/Excel not opened)
- **Quote:** "$580 per week" (metro), "$470 per week" (regional)
- **Establishes (measured outcome, bond-based):** Median weekly rent Sept qtr 2025: metropolitan Melbourne $580, regional Victoria $470. Annual change: Melbourne +3.5%, regional +4.6%. Quarterly change +1.1% both.
- **Does not establish:** anything after September 2025. The past-reports page (https://www.dffh.vic.gov.au/publications/past-rental-reports, read live) listed nothing later than Sept 2024, and no December 2025, March 2026 or June 2026 report could be located by search. **Whether a 2026 quarterly report has been published is not verified.** The LGA dataset on DataVic (https://discover.data.vic.gov.au/dataset/rental-report-quarterly-quarterly-median-rents-by-lga, indexed only) may carry newer quarters.
- Regional angle: regional rents grew faster than Melbourne in the year to Sept 2025 (4.6% vs 3.5%) on this official series.

### 1.2 REIV / PropTrack commentary (industry, corroboration)
- **Title:** "Victoria's housing market steady but softer on prices and rents" (Cameron Kusher column)
- **Publisher:** Real Estate Institute of Victoria (industry body)
- **Published:** 19 April 2026 (March 2026 data)
- **URL:** https://reiv.com.au/our-industry/news/cameron-kusher-april-2026
- **Read:** live
- **Quote:** "Regional Victoria has a lower rental vacancy rate than Melbourne albeit it is still somewhat elevated at 2.3 per cent"
- **Establishes (industry figure, not official):** March 2026: Melbourne median house rent $580/wk (flat year on year), unit $600 (+4.3%); regional median house rent $520 (+6.1% y/y), unit $420 (+7% y/y); vacancy Melbourne 2.5%, regional 2.3% (up from 2.1%). Note these are advertised-rent measures, not bond medians, so not directly comparable with 1.1.
- **Does not establish:** the underlying data provider is not stated on the fetched summary (likely PropTrack); treat as industry commentary.
- SQM Research national vacancy commentary for June/July 2026 (1.3%) seen **indexed only** via a third-party site; regional Victoria SQM figure **not verified**.

### 1.3 Anglicare Rental Affordability Snapshot 2026 (independent finding)
- **Title:** Victorian Rental Affordability Snapshot 2026 / news release "Finding an affordable rental 'a fairytale' for vulnerable Victorians"
- **Publisher:** Anglicare Victoria
- **Published:** 30 April 2026 (survey weekend March 2026)
- **URLs:** https://www.anglicarevic.org.au/research/victorian-rental-affordability-snapshot-2026/ ; https://www.anglicarevic.org.au/news/finding-an-affordable-rental-a-fairytale-for-vulnerable-victorians-new-report/
- **Read:** both live (PDF report not opened)
- **Quote:** "just 121 (0.8 per cent) individual properties were suitable for at least one household type living on income support"
- **Establishes (independent finding):** 15,244 listings surveyed (32 metro LGAs, 48 regional LGAs). 0 properties affordable for a single on JobSeeker/Youth Allowance; 1 for a single on DSP; 1 for a single parent on Parenting Payment. 30.9% (4,706) suitable for at least one minimum-wage household type (2025: 24%; 2024: 21.6%). **Regional Victoria:** 75 of 2,682 listings (3%) affordable for income-support households; 1,325 (49%) for minimum-wage households. Metro: about 46 of 12,562 (0.4%) for income support.
- **Does not establish:** actual rents paid; it is a one-weekend snapshot of advertised listings. Regional still far more affordable than metro for wage earners, but near-zero for income support.
- The Anglicare Australia national "Regional Reports" PDF (https://www.anglicare.asn.au/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Rental-Affordability-Snapshot-2026-Regional-Reports.pdf, read live) contained WA/NT/Qld sections only; no Victorian regional breakdown found there.

## 2. Supply

### 2.1 The target (promise)
- **Title:** "Affordability Partnership To Build 800,000 Victorian Homes"
- **Publisher:** Premier of Victoria (Andrews government)
- **Published:** 20 September 2023
- **URL:** https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/affordability-partnership-build-800000-victorian-homes
- **Read:** live
- **Quote:** "Unless we take bold and decisive action now, Victorians will be paying the price for generations"
- **Establishes (promise):** 800,000 homes over 10 years (80,000/yr); 2.24 million by 2051; regional and rural Victoria share stated as 425,600 homes (long-run to 2051, not annual). A target is not a completion.
- Plan for Victoria LGA housing targets (https://www.planning.vic.gov.au/planforvictoria/housing-for-all-victorians/housing-targets, read live, published 14 Feb 2025): to-2051 targets include Greater Geelong 128,600, Ballarat 46,900, Greater Bendigo 37,000, Wodonga 15,200, Greater Shepparton 15,250, Latrobe 13,000, Mildura 8,500, Warrnambool 7,200. Page says authorities "must implement the local government area housing targets" via planning scheme amendments. These are capacity targets, not builds.

### 2.2 Completions and approvals (measured outcome, ABS)
- **Title:** Building Activity, Australia, March 2026
- **Publisher:** Australian Bureau of Statistics
- **Published:** 8 July 2026
- **URL:** https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/industry/building-and-construction/building-activity-australia/latest-release
- **Read:** live (summary tables via fetch)
- **Quote:** "Total dwellings commenced fell 11.2% to 48,012 dwellings" (national)
- **Establishes:** Victoria March qtr 2026 (seasonally adjusted): 13,972 commencements, 14,519 completions. National completions 43,816. On a run-rate of ~14,000–14,500 per quarter Victoria is completing roughly 56,000–58,000 a year, well under 80,000. **Annual Victorian total for year to March 2026 not read directly from ABS tables — not verified.**
- Corroboration (**independent finding / industry**): Property Council Victoria, "New housing figures show Victoria falling behind New South Wales", 8 April 2026, https://www.propertycouncil.com.au/media-releases/new-housing-figures-show-victoria-falling-behind-new-south-wales (read live): "54,156" completions in calendar 2025, "a 10% drop versus 2024", about 26,000 short of 80,000. Earlier release (https://www.propertycouncil.com.au/media-releases/from-80000-homes-to-60000-victoria-falls-short-on-housing-targets-two-years-on, read live; page date shown as 19 Sept but year ambiguous) cited ~60,000 completions in 2024. The Urban Developer (**media**, 22 Jan 2026, https://www.theurbandeveloper.com/articles/abs-home-building-commencement-completions-september-2025, read live): Sept qtr 2025 Victorian commencements 13,415, "the lowest result for the quarter since 2014".
- **Title:** Building Approvals, Australia, July 2026 — ABS, released early September 2026, https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/industry/building-and-construction/building-approvals-australia/latest-release (read live). National total dwellings approved "fell 3.6% to 17,687" in July; Victoria rose 9.7% in the month (fetched figure 4,642 dwellings, treat as approximate); private houses in Victoria fell 4.1%. Approvals are not completions.

### 2.5 Big Housing Build ($5.3bn) (government figures)
- **Title:** "Big Housing Build Hits New Heights"
- **Publisher:** Premier of Victoria (Allan government)
- **Published:** 13 April 2026
- **URL:** https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/big-housing-build-hits-new-heights
- **Read:** live
- **Quote:** "The Liberals are blockers, Labor are builders"
- **Establishes (government figure):** "12,000" homes "completed or underway"; "7,000+" completed with tenants; combined Big Housing Build + Regional Housing Fund now "$6.3 billion" and "at least 13,300 homes". "Completed or underway" blends delivered with funded. Original 2020 promise: 12,000+ homes by 2026 (9,300 social) — the 2026 deadline is now being met by counting underway homes.
- **Independent scrutiny (media):** ABC, "Big Housing Build faced budget blowout in early days", 13 April 2026, https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-04-13/victorian-government-big-housing-build-budget-blowout-foi/106534886 (read live): FOI documents showed ~$1bn cost pressure by early 2023 ("would be a $6.261 billion business case if put forward today"); a cut option would have removed ~1,110 homes and "jeopardise regional investment targets"; government says on time and budget.
- **Regional Housing Fund** ($1bn, 1,300+ homes, to 2028): https://www.homes.vic.gov.au/regional-housing-fund (read live, undated): "more than 630 homes completed or under construction", "377 new and upgraded homes already completed" — of which only 90 are new homes plus 72 transitional and 3 First Nations; 187 are upgrades of existing stock. Named allocations: Geelong 72, Bendigo 56, Shepparton/Seymour/Rochester flood recovery 70, Colac-Otway 50, Wodonga 50, Hamilton 8. This is the key regional number: **as of the page date, fewer than 200 genuinely new regional homes finished under the $1bn fund.**
- 25% regional minimum spend for the Big Housing Build ($1.25bn) — well known but **not verified from a fetched page this session.**

## 3. Homelessness and social housing demand

### 3.1 AIHW Specialist Homelessness Services 2024–25 (measured outcome)
- **Title:** Specialist homelessness services annual report 2024–25, Client geography
- **Publisher:** Australian Institute of Health and Welfare
- **Published:** early 2026 (PDF version stamp 27 Feb 2026)
- **URL:** https://www.aihw.gov.au/reports/homelessness-services/specialist-homelessness-services-annual-report/contents/client-geography
- **Read:** live
- **Quote:** "Latrobe - Gippsland: 7,800 clients, or 251.2 per 10,000 population"
- **Establishes:** ~289,000 SHS clients nationally; national rate 106.3 per 10,000. The five SA4 regions with the most clients nationally were all Victorian: Melbourne-West 15,000 (157.2/10k), South East 10,400, Inner 9,700, **Latrobe-Gippsland 7,800 (251.2/10k)**, Outer East 6,800. Victoria's highest rate: **North West (283.2/10k)** (Mildura/Swan Hill region). Regional Victoria has rates well above Melbourne's and above the national rate.
- **Does not establish:** Victoria's statewide client total for 2024–25 (in a Tableau viz not readable; **not verified** — typically ~100,000+ in prior years).

### 3.2 Council to Homeless Persons on ROGS 2026 (independent finding, advocacy body citing Productivity Commission)
- **Title:** "Victoria's spending on social housing shrinks as unmet need soars"
- **Publisher:** Council to Homeless Persons
- **Published:** 30 January 2026
- **URL:** https://chp.org.au/article/victorias-spending-on-social-housing-shrinks-as-unmet-need-soars/
- **Read:** live
- **Quote:** "Cutting spending during the worst housing crisis in living memory is utterly staggering."
- **Establishes (from Report on Government Services 2026):** Victorian social housing spend fell 9% ($2.36bn to $2.16bn); $308.61 per capita vs national $397.47; social housing 2.88% of households (lowest of any state); 34.2% of people needing accommodation turned away; net +36 public housing dwellings over 2016–2025 while community housing +5,389; a quarter of greatest-need households waited 38+ months.
- Note: the "spend fell" figure reflects Big Housing Build capital tapering, which the government would contest; the ROGS source itself was not fetched.

## 4. Cost of living

### 4.1 Power Saving Bonus (delivered, closed)
- **Title:** "Real Power Bill Relief For Victorians"
- **Publisher:** Premier of Victoria
- **Published:** 27 January 2026
- **URL:** https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/real-power-bill-relief-victorians
- **Read:** live
- **Quote:** "claim their $100 Power Saving Bonus now before it closes on 31 March"
- **Establishes:** the 2025–26 round was $100, concession-card holders only, closed 31 March 2026; 245,000+ households paid ($24.5m+) at that date; since 2018 "$1 billion" across 4.5m applications (government figure). No new round announced in the 2026–27 Budget pages read. Status as at Sept 2026: closed.

### 4.2 2026–27 Budget cost-of-living measures (funded)
- **Title:** "Helping with the cost of living" (Budget 26/27 site) and "Labor Delivers Real Help To Ease The Cost Of Living" (Premier)
- **Publisher:** DTF / Premier of Victoria (Allan government; Treasurer Symes)
- **Published:** 5 May 2026
- **URLs:** https://www.budget.vic.gov.au/helping-cost-living ; https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/labor-delivers-real-help-ease-cost-living ; https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/labor-delivers-cost-living-help-and-surplus
- **Read:** all live
- **Quote:** "$759 million" for "20% rebate of up to $186 for people with one car"
- **Establishes (funded allocations, not yet all spent):** vehicle rego 20% rebate ($758.6m; up to $186/vehicle, $372 for two); public transport free to end May then half-price to 1 Jan 2027 ($278m; metro and V/Line; daily cap $5.70); $566m concessions incl. 17.5% electricity/gas concession; $5m Fair Fuel Plan; $120m family package (uniforms, glasses, breakfast clubs, zoo entry, Get Active vouchers); $26m emergency accommodation + $15m long-term housing (~$47m homelessness); $2.7m financial counselling; $11m food relief; budget surpluses claimed $700m (2025–26), $1bn (2026–27). Regional-specific items: $29.8m Shepparton line services, $317.7m V/Line reliability, $1bn+ road repairs.
- **Renter measures in budget:** none found beyond planning reform funding ($23.7m) and community housing energy upgrades ($10m). No "fair rent" or rent-cap measure. Land tax rates unchanged.

### 4.3 Property taxes
- **Stamp duty off-the-plan concession:** premier release 5 May 2026 and PwC alert (https://www.pwc.com.au/tax/tax-alerts/victorian-budget-2026-27.html, read live, professional-services source): concession for apartments/townhouses/units, no price cap, "contracts signed between 21 October 2024 and 21 April 2027" (extended six months in the budget). SRO page (read live) confirmed start 21 Oct 2024 only. Metro-skewed benefit; few off-the-plan strata sales in most regional towns.
- **First home buyer duty:** exemption under $600k / concession to $750k (since 2017) — SRO page indexed only, **not verified this session**. **Regional First Home Owner Grant** ($20,000 for new homes, 1 July 2017–30 June 2021) is listed under SRO "closed taxes, levies and grants" (https://www.sro.vic.gov.au/about-us/our-organisation/closed-taxes-levies-and-grants/regional-first-home-owner-grant, read live but body text not rendered): grant is closed; current statewide FHOG $10,000 for new homes — amount **not verified from a fetched page**.
- **Land tax / COVID Debt Levy (independent finding):** Victorian PBO, "Victorian land tax changes – COVID Debt Levy on Landholdings", January 2024 (requested by John Pesutto MP), https://static.pbo.vic.gov.au/files/PBO_Victorian-land-tax-changes_PUBLICATION.pdf (read live). Quote: "reduces the threshold for taxable landholdings from $300,000 to $50,000". Establishes: ~380,000 newly liable landowners paying ~$878 extra on average; 480,000 existing payers +$2,132 average; +$5,653m over 2023–24 to 2026–27; runs 1 Jan 2024–2033. PwC (read live): land tax revenue forecast $6.5bn in 2026–27; levy contributes ~$1.2bn in 2026–27, "set to last until 30 June 2033". Relevant to regional investors/holiday-house owners and, indirectly, rents.
- **"Highest-taxing state" claim:** The Mandarin (**media**, 27 April 2026, https://www.themandarin.com.au/311821-victoria-tops-the-tax-table-while-sinking-deeper-into-debt/, read live) reports ABS Taxation Revenue 2024–25 puts Victoria top on per-capita state tax and highest net debt per capita; **the per-capita dollar figure was not visible — not verified.** Grant Thornton and Liberal Party pages making the same claim seen indexed only.

### 4.4 Public transport fare cap
One line: regional fare cap (metro-price V/Line since March 2023) remains; on top, fares were free in May 2026 and half-price from 1 June 2026 to 1 January 2027 (budget, funded, see 4.2). Covered elsewhere.

### 4.5 Federal vs state split
- Housing Australia Future Fund, Help to Buy, National Housing Accord funding and Commonwealth Rent Assistance are **federal**. ABC (**media**, 4 Sept 2026, https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-09-04/commonwealth-invests-millions-victoria-crisis-housing/107111648, read live): $145m HAFF funding for 234 crisis/transitional homes in Melbourne, Greater Geelong, Mildura and Swan Hill, delivered with the Victorian government and community housing providers. Article cites providers: ~7,600 young Victorians without safe sleeping nightly; youth are 10% of homelessness population but 3% of beds (provider figures, not official).
- State controls: planning, stamp duty, land tax, rental law (Residential Tenancies Act), public housing, concessions, Power Saving Bonus, fares, rego.

## Gaps and research limits
1. **No 2026 Homes Victoria Rental Report located.** Latest official median rents are Sept qtr 2025. Before publishing, check DataVic LGA dataset or DFFH page for Dec 2025/Mar 2026/Jun 2026 releases.
2. **Victoria's annual ABS completions for year to March 2026 not read directly** from ABS tables; the 54,156 (calendar 2025) figure comes via the Property Council, an industry lobby. Verify against ABS Table 37/38 before quoting.
3. **AIHW Victoria statewide SHS client count** for 2024–25 sits in an unreadable Tableau; only SA4 figures captured.
4. **VHR waitlist** only to Sept 2025; a March 2026 or June 2026 quarter may exist.
5. **Big Housing Build regional split** (25% / $1.25bn commitment) and the 9,300 social/2,900 affordable breakdown were not re-verified this session.
6. Anglicare regional figures come from a news release, not the PDF report; confirm exact table values.
7. FHOG amounts and first-home-buyer duty thresholds on SRO pages did not render; verify before quoting.
8. No SQM regional-Victoria vacancy series fetched; only REIV commentary for March 2026.
9. NHSAC gives Victoria's Accord progress as a percentage only; the numeric state share is not on the page.
10. WebFetch summaries occasionally mis-stated dates (e.g. budget "2 May" vs "5 May"; Property Council release year). Dates above were cross-checked where two sources existed; treat single-source dates with caution.
