# Labor government positions on housing and cost of living, as at 15 September 2026

Covers the Allan government (to 28 July 2026) and the Carroll government (from 28 July 2026). Date checked for every source: **2026-09-15**. "Read live" = page fetched and read; "indexed only" = seen in search results only. Premier releases rarely name the Premier in the body; attribution below is by date (Allan until 28 July 2026, Carroll after). Labels: **promise**, **funded** (budget allocation), **delivered** (law in force / homes occupied), **government figure** (state-published count, not independently audited).

## 2. Housing supply: targets and progress claims

### 2.1 The standing target (promise, Sept 2023)
See baseline.md §2.1. 800,000 homes 2024–2034 (80,000/yr), 2.24m by 2051, 70% in established areas. The government has not withdrawn or revised the target as at Sept 2026; the Carroll-era release below still frames the opposition as "cutting 300,000 homes" from it.

### 2.2 Carroll government: townhouse fast-track claims
- **Title:** "More Homes Built Faster For Working People"
- **Publisher:** Premier of Victoria (Carroll government; Minister Kilkenny)
- **Published:** 2 September 2026
- **URL:** https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/more-homes-built-faster-working-people
- **Read:** live
- **Quote:** "Victoria continues to build and approve thousands of more homes than any other state"
- **Establishes (government figure):** Applications for up to four homes on a block are now to be assessed "in just 10 business days" (previously "more than 60 days"); townhouse/low-rise applications up "almost 50 per cent"; multi-dwelling permits in Melbourne's middle suburbs up "from around 2,000 to more than 3,100". Attack line: opposition would be "cutting 300,000 homes".
- **Does not establish:** any completions; permits are not homes. **Entirely metropolitan** — no regional Victoria content. "More than any other state" is contested by ABS/NHSAC data showing Victorian completions falling 9–10% (baseline §2.2–2.3).

### 2.3 Allan government: fast-track pathway
- **Title:** "Fast-Track Pathway Unlocks More Than 10,000 Homes"
- **Publisher:** Premier of Victoria (Allan government; Minister Kilkenny)
- **Published:** 16 March 2026
- **URL:** https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/fast-track-pathway-unlocks-more-10000-homes
- **Read:** live
- **Quote:** "Suburbs shouldn't be locked up for a lucky few"
- **Establishes (government figure):** 10,324 homes across 64 projects approved via the Development Facilitation Program since it opened to housing; 6,862 approved in 12 months to March 2026 vs 3,451 the year before; average 4 months; 20%+ affordable. "80% of homes located in Melbourne's inner and middle suburbs" — i.e. at most ~2,000 outside them, regional share not stated.
- Approvals, not builds.

### 2.4 Planning reforms in force (delivered as rules; outcomes pending)
- **Activity centres:** Premier release "We Need More Homes Close To Train Stations And Trams", 20 Oct 2024, https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/we-need-more-homes-close-train-stations-and-trams (read live): 50 train-and-tram-zone centres, "300,000+" homes by 2051. Planning Victoria program page (https://www.planning.vic.gov.au/guides-and-resources/strategies-and-initiatives/train-and-tram-zone-activity-centres/about-the-program, read live, updated 26 Aug 2026): "Plans for 60 centres have now been finalised"; development "over the next 20 to 30 years". All in Melbourne.
- **Townhouse and Low-Rise Code / ResCode overhaul:** The Urban Developer (**media**, 25 Feb 2025, https://www.theurbandeveloper.com/articles/victoria-housing-capacity-targets-inner-city-activity-centres-frankston, read live): townhouses up to three storeys deemed-to-comply, no third-party VCAT appeals, "1.45 million lots" eligible; first 10 of 50 activity centres named (Frankston, Broadmeadows, Camberwell Junction, etc.). Metro focus.
- **Plan for Victoria housing targets** (read live, Feb 2025): LGA targets to 2051 including regional cities (Geelong 128,600; Ballarat 46,900; Bendigo 37,000; Wodonga 15,200; Shepparton 15,250; Latrobe 13,000; Mildura 8,500; Warrnambool 7,200). Councils "must implement" via planning schemes. Regional relevance: this is the main regional housing-supply instrument, but it is a capacity target with no delivery mechanism or funding attached on the page.
- **Regional-specific planning:** No regional equivalent of the activity-centre program or the 10-day townhouse pathway was found in premier releases 2026. Regional planning content in 2026 releases is confined to Regional Housing Fund projects (§3.2).

### 2.5 Land release / state-led development
- "More New Homes For Melbourne's East", 19 June 2026, https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/more-new-homes-melbournes-east (read live): ~400 homes at Knoxfield (Ovata), sales from late 2026. Example of Development Victoria surplus-land projects; metro.

## 3. Social and affordable housing

### 3.1 Big Housing Build progress (government figure)
- **Title:** "Big Housing Build Hits New Heights"
- **Publisher:** Premier of Victoria (Allan government; Minister Shing then Housing)
- **Published:** 13 April 2026
- **URL:** https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/big-housing-build-hits-new-heights
- **Read:** live
- **Quote:** "For six years the Liberals and Greens have united to stop Labor's housing agenda at every turn."
- **Establishes:** "12,000" homes "completed or underway"; "7,000+" completed; program now "$6.3 billion" and "at least 13,300 homes" (BHB + Regional Housing Fund combined); 50,000+ jobs claimed. The 2020 promise was 12,000+ homes **by 2026**; as at April 2026 roughly 7,000 are finished. "Completed or underway" is the government's preferred metric. Independent context: ABC FOI story on ~$1bn cost pressure (baseline §2.5).

### 3.2 Regional Housing Fund (funded; partly delivered)
- **Title:** Regional Housing Fund
- **Publisher:** Homes Victoria
- **Published:** undated page (delivery plan Oct 2024)
- **URL:** https://www.homes.vic.gov.au/regional-housing-fund
- **Read:** live
- **Quote:** "more than 630 homes completed or under construction"
- **Establishes:** $1bn, "more than 1,300" homes, completion 2028; 377 "new and upgraded" completed, of which 90 new, 72 transitional, 3 First Nations, 187 upgrades. Named: Geelong 72, Bendigo 56, Shepparton/Seymour/Rochester 70 (flood recovery), Colac-Otway 50, Wodonga 50, Hamilton 8; 130 for First Nations Victorians. **This is the single most regionally relevant Labor housing program**, and the delivered new-home count is small relative to need (Latrobe-Gippsland alone had 7,800 homelessness-service clients in 2024–25).

### 3.3 Social Housing Growth Fund (funded, May 2026 budget)
- **Title:** "Budget Boom For Social Housing Growth"
- **Publisher:** Premier of Victoria (Allan government; Treasurer Symes, Minister Staikos)
- **Published:** 24 April 2026
- **URL:** https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/budget-boom-social-housing-growth
- **Read:** live
- **Quote:** "We're proudly building more homes than any other state or territory."
- **Establishes (funded, not delivered):** $860m top-up to the Social Housing Growth Fund to deliver "7,000+" social homes with community housing organisations; restates 12,000+ complete or underway and 7,000+ families housed. No regional allocation stated. Timeline for the 7,000 not on the page — **not verified**. Budget release (5 May, read live) restates "16,000+ social and affordable homes in progress" across all programs.

### 3.4 44 towers redevelopment
Homes Victoria update June 2025 (read live; baseline §2.6): first three towers relocations ~70% moved, ~20% preparing; South Yarra/Richmond next. Promise: "at least 10% more social housing" on the sites. No 2026 progress release found; Legislative Council inquiry recommended a pause (ABC, Dec 2025, indexed only). Politically contested; metro only.

### 3.5 Community housing energy upgrades (funded/delivered in part)
- **Title:** "More Affordable And Comfortable Community Houses"
- **Publisher:** Premier of Victoria (Allan government; Ministers D'Ambrosio, Staikos)
- **Published:** 30 June 2026
- **URL:** https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/more-affordable-and-comfortable-community-houses
- **Read:** live
- **Quote:** "Labor is driving down energy bills for Victorian community housing renters"
- **Establishes:** $9m grant round for 500+ community housing homes; overall $346.6m joint state–federal program for 23,000+ social housing homes (5,000 to all-electric); claims 21,000 renters already benefiting and ~$1,000/yr savings (government estimate). Framed as cost-of-living, not supply. No regional breakdown.

### 3.6 Federal partnership
ABC 4 Sept 2026 (**media**, read live): $145m HAFF for 234 crisis/transitional homes incl. **Greater Geelong, Mildura, Swan Hill**; delivered with Victorian government. This is federal money; the state's contribution is not stated in the article. Useful regional example but not a state commitment.

## 4. Renters' rights (delivered)

### 4.1 Reforms in force from 25 November 2025
- **Title:** "New Laws In Effect Today Strengthen Renter Rights"
- **Publisher:** Premier of Victoria (Allan government)
- **Published:** 25 November 2025
- **URL:** https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/new-laws-effect-today-strengthen-renter-rights
- **Read:** live
- **Quote:** "No-fault evictions are banned – meaning no Victorian renter can be kicked out of their home for no reason"
- **Establishes (delivered):** rental bidding banned; no-fault (end of first fixed term / no-reason) evictions abolished; 60→90 day notice for rent increases and certain notices to vacate; minimum standards must be met before advertising; annual smoke-alarm testing; renter data protection. Applies statewide, so fully relevant to regional renters.
- Original package announced 27 Nov 2024, "Crackdown On Excessive Rent Increases And Dodgy Rentals", https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/crackdown-excessive-rent-increases-and-dodgy-rentals (read live): also promised Rental Dispute Resolution Victoria and letting CAV rent-increase reviews consider the size of the increase and maintenance failures. **Whether RDRV is operating in 2026 not verified this session** (third-party summaries suggest further tranches in March 2026; indexed only).
- Not included anywhere: rent caps/freezes or limits on the size of increases. Tenants Victoria's 2026 platform (https://tenantsvic.org.au/election-2026/, read live) asks parties to "lock in" the reforms, extend minimum standards to the ~30% of leases pre-dating March 2021, and fund an "excessive rent increase checker"; no party responses recorded on the page.

### 4.2 Rental Taskforce
- **Title:** "Landlord crackdown: Victoria makes rental taskforce permanent"
- **Publisher:** Smart Property Investment (**media**, industry)
- **Published:** 31 August 2026
- **URL:** https://www.smartpropertyinvestment.com.au/tax-and-legal/28088-landlord-crackdown-victoria-makes-rental-taskforce-permanent
- **Read:** live
- **Quote:** "Renters should understand their rights, get help when they need it" (CAV director Nicole Rich)
- **Establishes (government figures via media):** since 2024 the CAV Rental Taskforce has inspected 600+ properties, assessed 2,600+ cases, issued 250+ warnings and 130 infringements (~$900,000) plus $1m+ penalties in the Ingenia Gardens matter; now made permanent with more inspectors and lawyers under the Carroll government. Original funding ($4m/yr, 2024) **not verified**. No regional inspection breakdown found; a regional angle to check is whether the taskforce operates outside Melbourne.

## 5. Cost of living (2026 measures)

### 5.1 Budget 2026–27 (funded; 5 May 2026; Allan/Symes)
Full list in baseline §4.2. Headline items the government campaigns on:
- 20% car rego rebate, $758.6m, up to $186 per vehicle (regional households with two cars: ~$372). Universal, so regionally significant given car dependence.
- Public transport free in May 2026, then half-price to 1 Jan 2027 ($278m), V/Line included; the March 2023 regional fare cap continues.
- $566m in concessions including the 17.5% energy concession; Power Saving Bonus $100 round closed 31 March 2026 (no new round announced).
- $47m homelessness ($26m emergency accommodation, $15m long-term housing).
- Budget claims surplus of $1bn in 2026–27 (government forecast).
- Sources read live: https://www.budget.vic.gov.au/helping-cost-living ; https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/labor-delivers-real-help-ease-cost-living ; https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/labor-delivers-cost-living-help-and-surplus. Quote: "trains, trams and buses – metro and V/Line – will be half-price every day until the end of the year".

### 5.2 Property tax settings the government defends
- Off-the-plan stamp duty concession extended to 21 April 2027 (budget; PwC read live). Primarily metro apartments/townhouses.
- COVID Debt Levy on land tax ($50k threshold) retained to 2033; ~$1.2bn in 2026–27 (PwC, read live; PBO Jan 2024 analysis of design). No Labor commitment to unwind it has been found. Opposition and industry attack this; regional holiday-home owners and small landlords are affected.
- No new first-home-buyer measure in the 2026–27 budget (PwC: "No dedicated first home buyer tax measures"). Regional FHOG ($20k) ended 30 June 2021 and has not been revived.

### 5.3 Carroll government cost-of-living posture (Aug–Sept 2026)
- No new state cost-of-living or housing spending measure announced between 28 July and 15 Sept 2026 was found on premier.vic.gov.au via search (the 2 Sept planning release and the Rental Taskforce permanence are administrative, not spending). The 4 Aug cabinet statement stresses value for money. Environment Victoria (advocacy, 30 July 2026, indexed only) urged Carroll not to "waver" on the clean-energy transition under cost-of-living pressure — suggests external expectation that gas/energy policy might be softened; **not verified**.
- Labor's May 2026 state conference (ABC **media**, 23 May 2026, https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-05-23/victorian-labor-and-liberal-state-conferences-election-agenda/106711922, read live): Allan's headline pledge was a $50m public apprenticeship academy (2,000 electrical placements) — a workforce, not housing, measure. Same article: PBO found Melbourne infrastructure investment per person "56.6% more than regions", and the Liberals pledged 25% of infrastructure funding to the regions. Labor had no matching regional pledge reported.

## 6. Election commitments so far (as at 15 Sept 2026)
- **Housing:** no discrete new Labor election policy found beyond the budget and existing programs. The government's campaign framing (2 Sept release) is "Labor builds, Liberals block / would cut 300,000 homes". viclabor.com.au policy pages were not located in search results — **not checked**.
