## 1. Taxes on property and households

### 1.1 Stamp duty — abolish for first home buyers up to $1m **[joint]**
- **Title/publisher/date/URL:** "Liberals and Nationals to abolish stamp duty for first homebuyers", Liberal Victoria, 26 May 2025, https://vicliberal.org.au/policies/2025-05-26-liberals-and-nationals-to-abolish-stamp-duty-for-first-homebuyers (Read — body text loaded on the vicliberal.org.au copy; the vic.liberal.org.au copy shows only a splash). Reaffirmed by Wilson: Hansard, Legislative Assembly, Appropriation (2026–2027) Bill 2026, 12 May 2026, https://www.parliament.vic.gov.au/parliamentary-activity/hansard/hansard-details/HANSARD-2145855009-35502 (Read). Nationals policy page, https://vic.nationals.org.au/policies/ (Read, undated).
- **Quote:** "Abolish stamp duty for first home buyers on all properties up to $1 million – established or off-the-plan" (Nationals policy page). Wilson (Hansard, 12 May 2026): first-home buyers "could save up to $55,000 in stamp duty".
- **Mechanism:** lift the existing full exemption (currently to $600,000, concession to $750,000) to a $1m full exemption, established or off-the-plan.
- **Cost:** Party release cites a PBO estimate of "over 17,000 exemptions in first full year"; no dollar cost on the party page. **media** ABC (27 May 2025, https://www.abc.net.au/news/2025-05-27/victorian-opposition-budget-reply/105343416, Read): opposition estimate "$1.09 billion over four-year government term"; Housing Minister Shing suggested >$2bn. The $1.09bn figure is a media report of an opposition figure, not a published party costing — treat as **not verified**.
- **Funding/offsets:** not stated for this measure. Newbury (May 2025, ABC): "We will make every announcement fully costed and released before the election."
- **Timing:** no start date published.
- **Regional distribution:** none stated; the $1m cap is state-wide (no separate regional threshold or restored regional First Home Owner Grant).
- **Unspecified:** start date, cost, whether the current $750k concession taper is retained above $1m, interaction with the federal 5% deposit scheme.
- Originally announced under Brad Battin; retained by Wilson (see 1.5 and Hansard 12 May 2026).

### 1.2 Land tax — lift the tax-free threshold back to $300,000 **[joint]**
- **Source:** Wilson budget reply, Hansard 12 May 2026 (URL above, Read); "Liberals, Nationals unveil 10-year economic plan", Liberal release republished by Mirage News, 8 May 2026, https://www.miragenews.com/liberals-nationals-unveil-10-year-economic-plan-1670059/ (Read; **media** mirror of a party release); Nationals policy page (Read).
- **Quote (Hansard):** "lifting the land tax threshold back to $300,000 ... by $50,000 every year for five years."
- **Mechanism:** reverse the 2023 COVID Debt Repayment Plan change that cut the general land tax threshold from $300,000 to $50,000; phased +$50,000 p.a. over five years. The Nationals page frames it as easing "upward pressure on rents".
- **Cost/funding:** not published. No PBO figure found (PBO tracker unreadable). **Not verified.**
- **Timing:** five-year phase-in; start year not stated.
- **Regional distribution:** none stated.
- **Unspecified:** whether the COVID debt levy's fixed-charge component ($500/$975) and the 0.1% surcharge on higher-value land are also removed; whether the trust threshold moves. Note: the phrase "COVID debt levy" does not appear in Wilson's reply; only the threshold restoration is promised. Nothing found on the payroll-tax COVID debt surcharge on large employers beyond the general payroll measures.

### 1.3 Short-stay levy (7.5% "Airbnb tax") — repeal **[joint]**
- **Source:** Nationals policy page lists repeal among taxes; 10-year economic plan release (8 May 2026, Mirage mirror, Read): "Repeal ... taxes on ... short-stay accommodation". **media** TA NEA, 21 Nov 2025, https://tanea.com.au/en/jess-wilson-vows-to-abolish-five-major-taxes-as-centerpiece-of-new-victorian-liberal-agenda/ (Read) lists the "Airbnb tax" among Wilson's five taxes to abolish. Earlier Nationals commitment: **media** Shepparton News, 23 Nov 2024, https://www.sheppnews.com.au/news/coalition-vows-to-repeal-victorias-short-stay-levy-2/ (Read).
- **Quote (Walsh, 2024, media):** short-stay providers used as "scapegoats for housing shortages".
- **Mechanism:** repeal the levy; no replacement regulation announced.
- **Cost:** not published. Levy revenue is hypothecated 25% to regional Victoria via Homes Victoria — the Coalition has not said how that regional social-housing stream would be replaced. **Not verified.**
- **Unspecified:** timing; treatment of council night-caps power; replacement funding for Homes Victoria.

### 1.4 Emergency Services and Volunteers Fund — repeal (one line; covered elsewhere) **[joint]**
Scrap the ESVF and "go back to the old fire services property levy" (O'Brien, **media** ABC 14 Jun 2026, https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-06-14/cfa-firefighters-volunteer-rally-protest-bendigo/106775498, Read; Nationals policy page, Read; First 100 Days release). Cost not published.

### 1.5 "Five taxes" framing (Nov 2025)
**media** TA NEA, 21 Nov 2025 (Read): Wilson named GP tax, ESVF, Airbnb tax, schools payroll tax and first-home-buyer stamp duty (to $1m) as the five taxes to go. Labor claimed a "$10.8 billion budget shortfall"; Wilson's costing not given. No party-site copy of this speech was found.

### 1.6 Cost-of-living headline figure
Wilson, Hansard 12 May 2026 (Read): "Our cost-of-living measures will save Victorian families $534 per year." No breakdown was given in the speech and none was found on the party sites. **Composition not verified.**

## 2. Housing supply and planning

### 2.1 "A new plan to restore dreams of home ownership" **[joint]**
- **Source:** Liberal Victoria media release, 25 Feb 2026, https://vicliberal.org.au/media-releases/2026-02-25-a-new-plan-to-restore-dreams-of-home-ownership (Read; the vic.liberal.org.au URL redirects here). **media** ABC, 25 Feb 2026, https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-02-25/victorian-opposition-unveils-home-ownership-housing-plan/106387550 (Read).
- **Quote (release):** "clear Labor's backlog of 27 Precinct Structure Plans". Southwick: "cut red tape, reduce costs and make it easier to build."
- **Mechanism (four planks):** (a) fast-track/clear the 27 PSPs in growth areas; (b) expand the Capital City Zone into inner suburbs (ABC: "Fishermans Bend, North Melbourne, Fitzroy and Collingwood") with heights stepping down toward established neighbourhoods; (c) restore council-led local structure plans in middle/outer suburbs — i.e. scrap Labor's activity-centre program; (d) "partner with councils to support sustainable expansion across regional Victoria" (ABC names Bendigo, Ballarat, Geelong).
- **Targets/cost:** none. No dwelling target, no statement on whether Labor's 2.24m-homes-by-2051 target or the council housing targets are kept. No funding figure.
- **Regional distribution:** generic; no regional PSP list or regional numbers.
- **Unspecified:** which 27 PSPs; delivery timeline; infrastructure funding for growth areas; whether the Townhouse and Low-Rise Code (VC267) survives.

### 2.2 Activity centres — scrap; return planning to councils **[joint]**
- **Source:** Hansard, Legislative Assembly, "Housing" (matter of public importance), 18 Mar 2026, https://www.parliament.vic.gov.au/parliamentary-activity/hansard/hansard-details/HANSARD-2145855009-34931 (Read). **media** ABC, 4 May 2026, https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-05-04/victoria-planning-reforms-activity-centres-resident-groups/106610900 (Read).
- **Quote (Southwick, Hansard):** "under a Jess Wilson government we will allow councils, through their structure plans, to build the housing". Newbury: "if you vote Liberal ... they will be ripped up." Southwick (ABC): "We will actually build as many if not more homes by having local council input."
- **Mechanism:** revoke the activity-centre controls (the Coalition already moved in the Legislative Council to revoke VC257/VC267/VC274 — lost 22–15, **media** Urbis, 16 May 2025, https://urbis.com.au/news/victoria-s-planning-reforms-narrowly-survive, Read — and voted against the Planning Amendment (Better Decisions Made Faster) Bill 2025, Dec 2025, **media** The Good Builder, 8 Dec 2025, https://thegoodbuilder.com.au/victorias-planning-standoff-what-the-jess-wilson-vote-reveals-about-the-states-housing-future/, Read).
- **Note on the townhouse code:** Southwick's 18 Mar 2026 remarks claim the townhouse code as originally a Coalition idea; no explicit commitment to keep or repeal it was found. **Unspecified.**
- **Sept 2026 addition:** Southwick told a developer forum the Coalition would scrap Labor's target of 70% of new homes in established areas and let "the market ... dictate that, not a government" (**media** ACM/Bendigo Advertiser, 2 Sep 2026, https://www.bendigoadvertiser.com.au/story/9342710/frosty-reception-as-major-parties-lock-horns-on-housing/, Read). No replacement split stated.

### 2.3 Geelong PSPs and developer-led PSPs
**media** Geelong Times, 6–7 Apr 2026, https://timesnewsgroup.com.au/geelongtimes/news/liberals-planning-election-commitments-to-geelong/ (Read): fast-track all existing Geelong PSPs; "reintroduce developer-led PSPs"; deliver roads/schools alongside housing. 25% of new state infrastructure spending to regional Victoria. Nationals release (O'Keeffe), 1 Jun 2026, https://vic.nationals.org.au/media-releases/kim-okeeffe-mp-regional-victorians-to-get-its-fair-share-under-new-guarantee/ (Read); **media** Stock & Land, 29 May 2026 (Read). ## 3. Social/public housing, homelessness, renters

- **Social housing:** no target, dollar commitment or program found on any party site, in the 10-year plan, in the budget reply, or in the First 100 Days plan. The Coalition's material is critical (Southwick, Hansard 18 Mar 2026: "36 homes in a decade" net public housing; O'Brien releases on vacant public housing, e.g. "147 social housing homes sitting empty", https://vic.nationals.org.au/media-releases/danny-obrien-mp-147-social-housing-homes-sitting-empty/, Indexed). Position on the 44-tower redevelopment: not found. **Research limit.**
- **Homelessness:** only a local candidate pledge found — $500,000 ($250k each to Fusion Mornington Peninsula and Southern Peninsula Community Support), Liberal candidate for Nepean, 30 Apr 2026 (**media** MPNEWS, https://www.mpnews.com.au/2026/05/13/500000-election-pledge-for-homelessness-services/, Read). No state-wide policy.
- **Renters:** no rental-reform platform found. Coalition members voted for leave to introduce the Greens' Rent Controls Bill on 1 Apr 2026 (Hansard, https://www.parliament.vic.gov.au/parliamentary-activity/hansard/hansard-details/HANSARD-2145855009-35166, Read) — a procedural vote, not support for rent caps; no Coalition speaker articulated a rental position. The only renter-framed claim is that lifting the land-tax threshold reduces "upward pressure on rents" (Nationals page). Tenants Victoria's election page records no Coalition response.

## 5. Fiscal frame, offsets and costings

- **10-year economic plan** (8 May 2026, Mirage mirror of Liberal release, Read; **media** Bellarine Times 14 May 2026, https://timesnewsgroup.com.au/bellarinetimes/news/coalition-target-debt-tax-in-state-budget-reply/, Read): cash surplus "within six years"/by 2032; payroll threshold to $1.1m in year one and $1.2m in year two (Hansard) and metro rate 4.8% from 2030–31.
- **Stated offsets:** scrap Treaty ("saving $1 billion", Hansard); cut total public sector by 2%; back-office hiring freeze; pause and review SRL (Bellarine Times; not in the Hansard reply); royal commission into Big Build. No offset is tied to any housing or tax measure.
- **Costings:** no consolidated Coalition costings document found. PBO election tracker unreadable. All dollar costs of the tax cuts are **not verified**.

## Gaps and research limits

1. No published Coalition costings for stamp duty, land tax, short-stay or ESVF repeal; PBO tracker and costing pages could not be read. The $1.09bn stamp-duty figure exists only in media.
2. No breakdown of the "$534 per year" household saving.
3. No social-housing target, public-housing maintenance/vacancy program, or position on the tower redevelopment found.
4. No state-wide homelessness policy found.
5. No renters' policy (rental standards, bond, no-grounds evictions) found; short-stay repeal is the only renter-adjacent measure and it removes a regional Homes Victoria revenue stream.
6. No regional First Home Owner Grant restoration, regional key-worker housing fund, or regional housing target found; regional housing content is limited to PSP fast-tracking (Geelong named) and "work with councils".
7. Activity-centre repeal is firm; the Townhouse and Low-Rise Code's fate and the 70/30 established/greenfield split's replacement are unspecified.
8. Land-tax pledge covers the threshold only; the COVID debt levy's fixed charges, surcharge and end-date are not addressed.
9. Infrastructure contributions (GAIC/ICP) reform: nothing beyond "developer-led PSPs".
10. Start dates: none of the housing/tax measures has a published commencement date except the payroll threshold (year one/year two) and land-tax phasing (five years).
11. Access: vic.liberal.org.au redirects to vicliberal.org.au; some legacy policy pages render empty and the "A Fresh Start" booklet is 404. jesswilsonkew.com.au yielded nothing. Hansard 12 May 2026 is the most authoritative primary record of Wilson's package.
