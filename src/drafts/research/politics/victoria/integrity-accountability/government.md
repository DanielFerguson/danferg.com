# Integrity: the Carroll government's position

Checked **15 September 2026**.

## The royal commission

- **ABC, "Ben Carroll calls a royal commission, but critics await details"**, 29 July 2026, https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-07-29/victoria-royal-commission-construction-big-build-ben-carroll/106972514 (live). Carroll promised "exhaustive terms of reference" and "no stone unturned" across contractors, subcontractors, labour hire, government and unions, breaking with Allan, who had refused an inquiry. Criticism at announcement: Jess Wilson called it a "fake royal commission" and said it should be set up before the election, noting no completion timing, no guaranteed reporting date and no assurance Labor ministers would be called. Former IBAC commissioner **Robert Redlich** warned that effectiveness depends on the terms of reference, since some governments call commissions with "very, very limited jurisdiction". Clancy Moore (Transparency International) and Geoffrey Watson SC supported it. Commissioner Christopher Kourakis was appointed 20 August; the commission reports in August 2027, after the election (part 4).

## The 8 September package

- **"More powers for construction royal commission"**, Premier of Victoria, 8 September 2026, https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/more-powers-construction-royal-commission (live). Creates Victoria's **first Office of the Special Prosecutor** with authority to prosecute; lets royal commissions compel written statements and information outside hearings; allows evidence-sharing with Victoria Police, IBAC and regulators. Carroll: "Victorians deserve the truth." Special Minister of State **Ingrid Stitt**: "These new powers will strengthen all Royal Commissions in this state." Corroborated by The Mandarin, "Victoria debates big corruption bills", 8 September 2026, https://www.themandarin.com.au/319794-victoria-debates-big-corruption-bills/ (partial, paywalled), which adds that non-compliance with compulsion outside hearings becomes a criminal offence and that the government is "racing against the caretaker timeline".
- **"More powers for IBAC to investigate corruption"**, Premier of Victoria, 8 September 2026, https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/more-powers-ibac-investigate-corruption (live). Follow-the-money powers over private and third-party subcontractors on government projects; a **lower threshold** so IBAC can investigate conduct that does not meet a criminal standard, including serious disciplinary matters and misconduct warranting termination; provisions allowing investigation of elected officials for serious breaches of conduct codes. Carroll: "In my Government, integrity is not optional."
- **What the bill actually does**, Ashurst, "IBAC's new powers: what they mean for the private sector", 15 September 2026, https://www.ashurstperkinscoie.com/en/insights/ibacs-new-powers-what-they-mean-for-the-private-sector/ (live; law-firm analysis). Extends jurisdiction to **associated entities** — contractors, subcontractors, agents, joint-venture partners and others performing public functions or handling public money — where previously IBAC could only investigate public bodies and public officers. Replaces "relevant offence" with conduct constituting "any criminal offence or a serious disciplinary matter", capturing grey corruption. Clarifies that a benefit need not actually be obtained. Commences the day after assent, with transitional provisions applying to conduct on or after commencement **plus certain pre-commencement conduct not previously dismissed or completed**.
- **Origin**: announced by Allan on 1 June 2026 as "the most far-reaching overhaul of IBAC's powers since it was established in 2012", including retrospective application to recent allegations — ABC, https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-06-01/extended-powers-for-anti-corruption-ibac-victorian-government/106746190 (live). Wilson's response then: the government wanted "the headline today and the investigation after the election". Geoffrey Watson SC: a positive step, but "could have been done in February when the report was released".

## The carve-out

- **AAP (Melissa Meehan and Allanah Sciberras) via ACM**, "Reforms blocking Big Build corruption probe under fire", 8 September 2026, https://www.thecourier.com.au/story/9346058/reforms-blocking-big-build-corruption-probe-under-fire/ (live). A late amendment, circulated at 6pm on the Monday, prevents IBAC from investigating corruption in **already-addressed** government construction matters — which covers the organised-crime allegations referred to IBAC two years earlier by Allan and not taken up. Carroll's position: the royal commission's scope makes a fresh referral unnecessary, and investigations could still proceed on a new referral or new information; he would not confirm making one. Carroll describes the package as "the biggest reforms to integrity in Victoria in a generation". Wilson: "This government is not serious about getting to the bottom of the largest corruption scandal in this state's history." Greens leader **Ellen Sandell** called the rushed process "very fishy".
- **Status as at 15 September 2026**: Independent Broad-based Anti-corruption Commission Amendment Bill 2026 passed the Legislative Assembly on 10 September (first reading 8 September) and sits at second reading in the Legislative Council, https://www.legislation.vic.gov.au/bills/independent-broad-based-anti-corruption-commission-amendment-bill-2026 (live). **Not law when checked.**
