# Integrity: Coalition position

Checked **15 September 2026**.

## What is on the record

- **Called for the inquiry first.** The Coalition pressed for a royal commission into Big Build corruption before the government acted, and when Carroll called one on 29 July, Jess Wilson called it a "fake royal commission", saying it should be established before the election and objecting that there was no completion timing, no guaranteed reporting date and no assurance Labor ministers would be called as witnesses (ABC, 29 July 2026).
- **On the 1 June IBAC announcement**: "the headline today and the investigation after the election" (ABC, 1 June 2026).
- **On the September bill**: "This government is not serious about getting to the bottom of the largest corruption scandal in this state's history" (AAP/ACM, 8 September 2026).
- **Budget-process integrity**, from the 10-year economic plan of 8 May 2026 (part 10): a **Charter of Budget Honesty** and a **real-time expenditure tracker**; a **royal commission** to end what it calls "$15 billion of Big Build corruption"; and a **new construction sector watchdog**. These are the Coalition's own institutional commitments and they are real, if unpriced.
- **Fiscal transparency as an integrity claim** is the through-line: the Charter answers the undisclosed fare levy and the overstated Suburban Rail Loop savings more directly than anything Labor has offered.

## What was searched for and not found

No Coalition position was located on: IBAC's funding or the three agencies' joint February ask; the "exceptional circumstances" threshold for public hearings; the definition of corrupt conduct; commissioner appointments; freedom of information reform; political donations or expenditure caps; the lobbyist register or post-ministerial employment; whistleblower protections; or public service politicisation. Searches covered vicliberal.org.au (which renders poorly), vic.nationals.org.au/policies (live, no integrity section beyond the construction watchdog), and news coverage. **This is a retrieval limit, and it is also a fair observation that the Coalition's integrity offer is about this scandal and the budget process rather than about the watchdogs' own powers.**

## Gaps and research limits

1. No Coalition integrity policy document was located; the record is media quotes plus the economic plan's institutional items.
2. The "$15 billion" figure traces to the Watson review estimate the CFMEU administrator redacted as untested (part 5).
3. Whether the Coalition would keep the IBAC bill's expanded powers if it passes, or reintroduce them if it doesn't, was not found.
4. Hansard for the bill's Assembly stages (8–10 September) was not read; the Coalition's stated reasons for its vote are therefore not captured.
