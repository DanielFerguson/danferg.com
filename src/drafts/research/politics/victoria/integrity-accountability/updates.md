# Part 11: progression, corrections and review

## 15 September 2026 — first draft

- Written around a live bill. The Independent Broad-based Anti-corruption Commission Amendment Bill 2026 passed the Assembly on 10 September and was at second reading in the Council when checked; the draft states the stage and warns a later reader to check it first.
- The government's package is given its substance before its problem: royal commission (29 July), Office of the Special Prosecutor and compulsion outside hearings (8 September), and an IBAC jurisdiction extended to associated entities with the criminal-offence threshold replaced by "any criminal offence or a serious disciplinary matter" — which is the gap that produced Operation Daintree's no-corrupt-conduct finding in part 5.
- The carve-out is sourced twice and disclosed as not read directly: AAP's report of the 6pm amendment barring already-addressed construction matters, and Ashurst's summary of transitional provisions excluding pre-commencement conduct previously dismissed or completed. The June announcement's description of the powers as reaching recent allegations is set beside it.
- Carroll's defence (the royal commission covers the ground; a new referral could still start an investigation) is stated before it is answered with the August 2027 reporting date.
- Recorded as not found: any Coalition position on IBAC funding, the hearings threshold, corrupt-conduct definition, appointments, FOI, donations, lobbying or whistleblowers; any One Nation integrity policy at all; any party response to the three watchdogs' February funding ask.
- The public-hearings threshold and the funding process are identified as the two things the bill does not touch, and become two of the three questions for candidates.
