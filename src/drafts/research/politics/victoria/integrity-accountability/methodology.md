# Part 11: research method and traceability

Checked 15 September 2026, Australia/Melbourne. Scope is what each party would change about the integrity system — IBAC's jurisdiction, powers, funding and hearings; the Ombudsman and Auditor-General; freedom of information; donations and lobbying; and the documentation and disclosure practices that make delivery auditable. Part 5 covers what past investigations found; this part does not re-litigate them, it carries them as the reason the machinery matters.

Researched inline by the writer rather than through parallel passes, as with part 10, because the material is concentrated and moving: the central bill was introduced on 8 September and was still before the Legislative Council on the day of writing.

Pages fetched and read: the Centre for Public Integrity's IBAC reform paper and its assessment of the June political finance bill; the integrity agencies' joint funding statement; OVIC's 2024–25 annual report summary; the ABC reports of 1 June, 29 July and the AAP/ACM report of 8 September; the Premier's two releases of 8 September; the Ashurst analysis of the bill; The Mandarin's account of the same day; the bill's status page on legislation.vic.gov.au; the Greens' democracy and integrity policy; and Transparency International Australia's asks.

Evidence rules as in earlier parts, with three specific to this one. A bill before parliament is not law, and the draft says so and names the stage. The effect of the late amendment is taken from two sources that agree — the AAP report and the bill's transitional provisions as summarised by Ashurst — because the amendment text itself was not read, and the draft discloses that. And the government's own argument for the carve-out (that the royal commission covers the ground) is stated as a real argument before it is answered, rather than omitted.

The article's structural choice is to give the government's package its full weight before turning to the carve-out, and to end by refusing to net the two against each other. The regional section exists because integrity reads as a city preoccupation otherwise; it builds a chain from documentation failures in earlier parts to the watchdogs that found them.

Writing constraints: first person, Dan's stated position, no invented detail; one `AUTHOR:` comment asking whether integrity moves his vote on its own or only when it shows up as something practical, which is the question the article's last section argues about. Same five questions, plus three specific questions for candidates. No numerical party score.
