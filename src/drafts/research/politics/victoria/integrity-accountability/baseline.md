# Integrity and competent government: baseline

Research notes for part 11. All sources checked **15 September 2026**. "Live" = fetched and read; "indexed" = seen only in search results.

## 1. What IBAC could and couldn't do before 2026

- **The Centre for Public Integrity, "Sharpening the watchdog's teeth: how to strengthen the Victorian IBAC"**, 10 August 2022, https://publicintegrity.org.au/research_papers/victorian-ibac-reform/ (live). The structural criticisms: public hearings are allowed only in "exceptional circumstances", which courts read as "rare and unusual" and which has no equivalent in the NSW ICAC; IBAC can only pursue conduct amounting to a criminal offence and must "suspect on reasonable grounds" before it starts, which blocks early inquiry; and commissioner appointments run through government-dominated oversight committees. Five reforms urged, including lowering the hearings threshold, broadening the definition of corrupt conduct, and non-executive control of appointments. **Independent advocacy; 2022, so pre-dates the 2026 bill — used for the structural picture, not for current law.**
- These constraints are visible in the findings themselves (part 5): Operation Watts could not recommend prosecution given the difficulties of proof and the state of the law; Operation Daintree found improper influence but made no corrupt-conduct finding because Victorian law reserves the term for conduct that would be a crime.

## 2. Funding independence

- **"Victoria's integrity agencies renew call for transparent, independent funding"**, IBAC, Ombudsman and VAGO jointly, 18 February 2026, https://www.ibac.vic.gov.au/Victorias-integrity-agencies-renew-call-for-transparent-independent-funding (live). Quote (Victorian Ombudsman): "The current funding processes are opaque and shrouded in secrecy." Twelve proposed reforms across information access (written explanations of funding decisions, post-budget briefings), parliamentary oversight (briefing committees before decisions), and administrative safeguards (exemption from efficiency dividends, a contingency reserve, a published funding protocol). IBAC Commissioner **Victoria Elliott**: "Even the perception of corruption can undermine public trust." **No dollar figures in the release.** **Independent agencies, joint statement.** This is the ask that no party has answered.

## 3. Political finance

- **Centre for Public Integrity on the Victorian political finance bill**, 3 June 2026, https://publicintegrity.org.au/media-release-victorian-political-finance-bill-important-first-step-broader-reform-needed/ (live). The bill reinstates a donation cap of **$7,500 per election period**, restores 21-day disclosure, expands public funding, and removes the nominated-entity arrangements at the heart of a High Court decision. The Centre calls it "an emergency repair job, not a long-term solution" and wants evidence-based donation and expenditure caps and real-time disclosure, plus treatment of fundraising and self-funded campaigns; it welcomes an independent review after the 2026 election. **Independent advocacy.**

## 4. Freedom of information

- **OVIC annual report 2024–25**, tabled 14 November 2025, https://ovic.vic.gov.au/newsitem/ovics-2024-2025-annual-report-tabled-in-victorian-parliament/ (live). FOI requests across Victoria reached **58,181 in 2024–25**, up 9.4% and another record; privacy complaints up 51% to 162; security incident notifications up 50% to 1,094. Information Commissioner Sean Morrison points to audits and investigations as the regulatory focus. **Timeliness and agency compliance rates were not on the page and are not verified.**

## 5. Transparency of delivery

Established elsewhere in the guide and carried here: the Auditor-General found the Commonwealth Games business case understated costs and overstated benefits and that agencies failed to give frank and timely advice (part 5); the undisclosed 1% rail charge on fares, introduced in January 2025 and never disclosed in a budget paper or release, was found by the Auditor-General in August 2026 and abolished, while the government's claimed $1bn of Suburban Rail Loop savings covered items never in the estimate (parts 5 and 10); the Ombudsman's 2023 finding of creeping politicisation of the public service, while not substantiating the claim that it had been stacked with party operatives (part 5).
