# Integrity: crossbench, minor parties and civil society

Checked **15 September 2026**.

## Greens

- **Democracy and integrity policy**, Australian Greens Victoria, last updated 15 May 2026, https://greens.org.au/vic/policies/democracy-and-integrity-policy (live). Commitments: "Increase funding to integrity bodies, such as IBAC, to ensure they are not forced to limit their inquiries due to resource constraints"; "Remove the limits on public hearings for integrity bodies"; ban political donations "from for-profit businesses and their directors" and cap donations at state and local level; a **five-year cooling-off period** before ministers, parliamentary secretaries and senior staff can lobby; "Strengthen the public's right to information by removing commercial-in-confidence exemptions from freedom of information requests"; and parliamentary reforms letting MPs debate amendments and ask questions on the substance of all bills, and requiring governments to produce documents requested by Parliament in a timely fashion. **The most complete integrity platform of any party located in this research, and uncosted.**
- On the September bill, leader **Ellen Sandell** called the rushed process "very fishy", objecting to a bill delivered at 6pm on a Monday (AAP/ACM, 8 September 2026).
- The Greens have pressed for broadened anti-corruption powers through 2026, including after the February revelation that the Premier's office knew in 2024 that IBAC had declined a CFMEU referral (ABC, 17 February 2026, https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-02-17/greens-call-broadened-powers-anticorruption-body-cmfeu/106350798, indexed).

## One Nation and other minor parties

No integrity or anti-corruption policy was located for One Nation Victoria, the Libertarians, Legalise Cannabis, Animal Justice, Shooters Fishers and Farmers, Family First or the Victorian Socialists. **Research limit, not a finding.**

## Civil society

- **Transparency International Australia**, "Strong royal commission and IBAC reforms key to win back Victorians' trust", 28 July 2026, https://transparency.org.au/strong-royal-commission-and-ibac-reforms-key-to-win-back-victorians-trust/ (live). Asks: the broadest possible scope; examine organised crime in the CFMEU and the sector; examine the relationship between government, union and criminal actors; scrutinise whether the Allan government ignored or enabled corruption; assess weaknesses in IBAC and police; and recommend prevention. On IBAC: implement follow-the-money powers, broaden authority below criminal thresholds, strengthen prevention. CEO **Clancy Moore**: "Only a Royal Commission with broad terms of reference and strong coercive powers will truly uncover what went wrong."
- **The Centre for Public Integrity**: the structural IBAC critique (baseline §1) and the political finance assessment (baseline §3).
- **Robert Redlich**, former IBAC commissioner, on terms of reference determining whether a commission is real (ABC, 29 July 2026).

## Regional independents

No integrity or transparency statement was found for Lachlan McKeeman (Benambra), Steve Manning (Ovens Valley) or Ron Nelson (South Barwon). Note that "Voices"-style independents elsewhere have often run on integrity; that is a general observation, not evidence about these candidates.
