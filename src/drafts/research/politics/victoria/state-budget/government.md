# The state budget: Labor government positions

Checked **15 September 2026**. Covers the Allan government to 28 July 2026 and the Carroll government after it.

## The fiscal narrative

Symes's 5 May budget is built on one claim: the first operating surplus in seven years, $1.048bn in 2026–27, and "the only surplus on the eastern seaboard" (AAP, 5 May 2026). The five-step strategy on budget.vic.gov.au places the state at step three of five, with net debt stabilising as a share of GSP from 2026–27 and falling thereafter. Cost-of-living measures are the political front end (part 7): the registration rebate, half-price fares to 1 January, concessions.

What the government does not put in its own framing: cash deficits of more than $30bn across the four years (AAP), and the Auditor-General's finding of an accrual operating **loss** of $2.6bn in 2024–25 with cash deficits every year since 2016–17 (VAGO, 24 November 2025). Both can be true — the surplus is a forecast for a later year on an accrual measure — and the article states both rather than choosing one.

## Carroll's posture since 28 July 2026

- Cabinet statement, 4 August 2026, https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/statement-premier-173 (live, cited in part 7 research): cost of living named first among three priorities; a smaller cabinet "to save taxpayer money"; "Every dollar my government spends will be treated with respect". Treasurer is **Colin Brooks** (replacing Symes, who moved to Energy).
- **Suburban Rail Loop**: cabinet review announced late August; the undisclosed 1% fare levy abolished; $1bn of "savings" claimed that VAGO says were never in the estimate (part 5's `abc-srl`, and baseline §3). This is the clearest Carroll-era fiscal decision and it cuts both ways: the levy's abolition is a transparency win; the savings claim is not one.
- **Western Renewables Link** paused 15 September pending an independent costing due by the end of the year (part 8). A cost review, not a cancellation, and the second project in three weeks to be re-examined on cost grounds.
- No new spending program of any size has been announced by the Carroll government up to 15 September, on housing, energy or roads. No Labor election platform or costings process had been published. **Absence of evidence at the date checked, not evidence of absence.**

## What Labor defends rather than advertises

The COVID Debt Repayment Plan: the land-tax threshold cut from $300,000 to $50,000 and the payroll surcharge, running to 2033, raising $5,653m over 2023–24 to 2026–27 on the PBO's January 2024 analysis (part 7). No commitment to unwind it early has been found. The government's attack on the Coalition rests on the same ground: the October 2025 release "Cuts Only Way To Deliver Liberals $10.8 Billion Blackhole", https://www.premier.vic.gov.au/cuts-only-way-deliver-liberals-108-billion-blackhole (live), lists five unfunded commitments (fee freeze, first-home-buyer stamp duty, ESVF repeal, short-stay levy repeal, non-government schools payroll exemption) and claims they would mean sacking "up to 4,500 police officers, 9,000 teachers and 18,000 nurses". **The release does not show the arithmetic**, and the figure predates Wilson's 10-year plan, so the article treats it as a political claim with a named composition and no published workings.

## Regional

Budget regional measures are listed in baseline §1. The government has not answered the PBO's per-person investment finding with a regional allocation rule of its own, and has no equivalent of the Coalition's Fair Share Guarantee (part 6). Its regional pitch is the aggregate: "more than $50 billion in regional Victoria" over twelve years.
