# The state budget: crossbench, minor parties and the regional lens

Checked **15 September 2026**. Silence in the sources found is a research limit, not a finding.

## Greens

Revenue measures published in 2026 are the investor land tax on residential holdings above $5m, at 5.3%, with the party's own two figures — "$6.4 billion over the decade" in the 9 July release and "approximately $2 billion over the next four years" on the platform page (part 7's `greens-housing-jul-2026` and `greens-platform-housing`). Spending commitments attached to it: stamp duty abolition to $1.1m sliding to $1.6m, and more than 88,000 public homes from a $4bn initial investment. Energy commitments rest on the 2022 PBO costing ($10bn public renewables to 2030; about $2.19bn to get a million homes off gas) with no 2026 restatement (part 8's `pbo-greens-2022`). No published Greens position on net debt, the cash-deficit series or the COVID debt levy was found.

## One Nation

The energy policy would end new renewable subsidies and abolish renewable energy zones; its leader said of the cost of keeping Loy Yang A open past 2035, "Those figures haven't been discussed just yet" (part 8's `abc-onenation-loy-yang`). Its cost-of-living page pairs ending subsidies with removing insurance duty. **No fiscal aggregate, debt position or costing of any kind was located.**

## Other parties

Victorian Socialists propose a State Bank of Victoria and a publicly owned builder, uncosted (part 7's `sv-housing`). The Libertarians' "Dump Net Zero" page claims a "$1 billion corporate energy welfare program" to cut (part 8 research). Nothing fiscal was found for Legalise Cannabis, Animal Justice, Shooters Fishers and Farmers or Family First.

## The regional lens

- The PBO's per-person finding and its reversal when five major projects are excluded (baseline §2) is the central regional fact, and neither major party quotes both halves. The Coalition's Fair Share Guarantee answers the first half; nothing answers the second.
- **Rural Councils Victoria 2026 platform**, https://ruralcouncilsvictoria.org.au/vicvotes2026/ (live): a $1bn Rural and Regional Productivity and Growth Fund with at least 25% guaranteed for rural Victoria; four streams (economic development, community assets, council sustainability, housing and enabling infrastructure); road funding kept separate, with state-road maintenance, freight performance and an extension of the Safe Local Roads and Streets Program past 2027.
- Regional budget allocations for 2026–27 are listed in baseline §1. The emergency-services levy's regional incidence is part 3; regional road maintenance against the $3bn-a-year business case is part 2.

## The range of views on debt

Worth presenting as a range rather than a verdict:
- **VAGO** (independent): debt growth has outpaced revenue and economic growth; interest at 6.7% of revenue rising to $10.6bn by 2028–29; "persistent deficits highlight the state's reliance on debt" (baseline §2).
- **Saul Eslake** (economist, quoted AAP 5 May 2026): "until you start running cash surpluses, you can't begin to repay debt."
- **S&P's Rebecca Hrvatin** (quoted ABC 5 May 2026): "The risk is that it becomes unsustainable and there is no, at the moment, path to repayment of that debt." The rating itself remains AA stable, Aa2 stable (DTF, October 2025).
- **Prof. David Hayward, RMIT** (quoted ABC 5 May 2026), on the political economy rather than the arithmetic: "When they get extra cash in they spend it."
- The government's own position: step three of five, surpluses from 2025–26, debt stabilising as a share of GSP.

No source located in this research calls Victoria's position a solvency crisis; the disagreement is about the path to repayment and the opportunity cost of interest.
