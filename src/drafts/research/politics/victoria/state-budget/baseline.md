# The state budget: baseline (official and independent figures)

Research notes for part 10. All sources checked **15 September 2026**. "Live" = page fetched and read; "indexed" = seen only in search results. Labels: **promise / funded / delivered / measured outcome / government figure / independent finding**.

## 1. The 2026–27 Budget, 5 May 2026 (Treasurer Jaclyn Symes)

- **Fiscal strategy page**, budget.vic.gov.au, https://www.budget.vic.gov.au/delivering-our-fiscal-strategy (live). Quote: "operating surplus of over $700 million in 2025-26, $1 billion in 2026-27, increasing to $2 billion in 2029-30"; net debt "falling as a share of the economy from 2026-27 onwards". The five-step strategy: growth and jobs → operating cash surplus → operating surpluses → stabilise net debt as a share of GSP → reduce it. **Government figure / promise.** Does not establish cash position; the page carries no net-debt dollars or interest figures.
- **ABC budget wrap**, 5 May 2026 (updated 6 May), https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-05-05/victorian-state-budget-2026-wrap/106638608 (live). Surplus $1.048bn in 2026–27, "first in seven years"; projected net debt $199.3bn by 2029–30; a further $40bn of borrowing over four years; interest $24.4m a day now, $32.3m a day and over $11bn a year by 2029–30. Symes quote: "We have continued to fund the frontline services." **Media, reporting budget papers.**
- **AAP via ACM (Callum Godde)**, 5 May 2026, https://www.thecourier.com.au/story/9239642/state-budget-2026-victorias-debt-to-reach-200-billion-by-2030/ (live). Net debt $175.6bn in 2026–27, 24.9% of GSP, and $199.3bn by mid-2030; interest $11.8bn a year by 2030; the 2026–27 surplus revised down from $1.9bn to about $1bn; four-year surplus total $6.8bn; **cash deficits of more than $30bn across the four years**. Symes: "the only surplus on the eastern seaboard." Saul Eslake: "until you start running cash surpluses, you can't begin to repay debt." **Media + named economist.** The accrual-surplus / cash-deficit distinction is the single most load-bearing fact in this article.
- **Tax mix**, Pitcher Partners analysis, 5 May 2026, https://www.pitcher.com.au/insights/victorian-state-budget-2026-27-analysis/ (live; professional-services source). Land tax $7.6bn in 2026–27; payroll tax about $12.5bn; land transfer duty $10.0bn in 2026–27 rising to $12.4bn by 2029–30; total tax collections above $50bn a year by 2030. Its characterisation of a burden "borne by Victorians, particularly property owners, investors and developers" is commentary, not a measurement. COVID debt levy receipts not stated here (PBO's January 2024 analysis, already cited in part 7, gives the design and the $5,653m over 2023–24 to 2026–27).
- **Regional measures**, budget.vic.gov.au, https://www.budget.vic.gov.au/regional-victoria-place-be (live): $481m regional transport services, $318m regional rail upgrades, $127m freight rail, $277m regional education and health, $75m Regional Health Infrastructure Fund, $95m "stronger regions", $84m agriculture, $95m to upgrade nine schools, $73m road upgrades, $23m Future Regions. Claim: "more than $50 billion in regional Victoria" over twelve years. **Government figures; allocations, not outcomes.** Note the $1bn-plus road repair line reported elsewhere is the statewide figure, not regional-only.

## 2. Independent scrutiny

- **Auditor-General's Report on the Annual Financial Report of the State of Victoria 2024–25**, tabled 24 November 2025, https://www.audit.vic.gov.au/report/auditor-generals-report-annual-financial-report-state-victoria-2024-25 (live). Unqualified audit opinion. General government net operating **loss of $2.6bn** in 2024–25 (better than $4.2bn, but $400m worse than budget); accumulated losses over six years $50.6bn; operating cash surplus $3.2bn. Gross debt $187.9bn, up from $168.8bn, projected $236.6bn by June 2029; highest debt-to-GSP of any state at about 30%. Interest expense **$6.8bn, or 6.7% of revenue**, forecast $10.6bn by 2028–29. Fiscal cash deficits every year since 2016–17. Quotes: "debt growth has consistently outpaced GGS revenue and state economic growth"; "persistent deficits highlight the state's reliance on debt". **Independent finding.** This is the strongest single source for the article.
- **PBO, Regional and metropolitan investment: 2025–26 Budget**, prepared 10 November 2025, requested by Danny O'Brien MP, https://static.pbo.vic.gov.au/files/PBO_Regional-and-metropolitan-investment-2025-26_PUBLIC.pdf (live). Quote: "Asset investment per person in metropolitan Victoria ($19,338) is 56.6% more than in regional Victoria ($12,348)." **But**: excluding five major projects, metropolitan drops to $9,632 per person, "22.0% lower than in regional Victoria". Regional projects are 48.4% of the 1,414 projects identified but 12.0% of total asset investment; metropolitan 31.6% of projects and 57.6% of investment. Measure is total estimated investment — lifetime capital expenditure, not annual spend. **Independent finding, with a reversal that both sides omit.**
- **Credit ratings**, DTF, https://www.dtf.vic.gov.au/state-credit-rating (live, page updated 28 October 2025): "AA with a stable outlook by S&P" and "Aa2 with a stable outlook by Moody's". No 2026 agency action was found; a search result suggesting a Moody's downgrade over refinancing risk could not be dated or verified and is **not used**. S&P analyst Rebecca Hrvatin, quoted in the ABC wrap: "The risk is that it becomes unsustainable and there is no, at the moment, path to repayment of that debt." **Analyst comment, not a rating action.**
- **PBO 2026 Budget Snapshot and 2026 Election Commitment Tracker** (https://pbo.vic.gov.au/2026BudgetSnapshot, https://pbo.vic.gov.au/2026_election_commitment_tracker) are JavaScript applications that return no readable content to this tool. **Not read — a retrieval limit, not an absence.**

## 3. The Suburban Rail Loop

- **VAGO report on SRL East**, reported 26 August 2026, https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-08-26/victorian-auditor-general-report-on-srl/107079060 (live). The project is "more likely than not to exceed its publicly disclosed cost"; station procurement about a year late; early works six months late; significant contingency already spent. The undisclosed **1% "rail improvement charge"** on fares, introduced at the start of 2025 and hidden inside inflation-based fare rises, was projected to raise about $8bn over 37 years to 2062, about $4.8bn of it for SRL East — "the project's largest source of value capture revenue" — and by August had raised about $6.5 million. Original SRL East estimate $34.5bn; Commonwealth commitment $6bn; a third of funding to come from value capture.
- **Carroll's response**, 26–27 August 2026, https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-08-27/suburban-rail-loop-victoria-cost-cuts-questioned/107080970 (live; already cited in the guide as `abc-srl`). Levy abolished — "there was no transparency in its creation" — and $1bn of "savings" claimed by cutting two underground interchanges and a car park, which VAGO says were **never in the original estimate** (the interchanges would have cost $1.7bn against $104m budgeted; the car park $137m against $84m).

## 4. Other calls on the budget already established elsewhere in the guide

Roads: the department's own business case says holding pavement condition costs more than $3bn a year, against about $1.39bn allocated for 2026–27 after the August top-up (part 2). Emergency services: the levy is forecast to raise about $1.6bn gross in 2026–27 (part 6, Budget Paper 5). Housing: $860m added to the Social Housing Growth Fund, $47m for homelessness (part 7). Energy: no new household energy rebate, the last Power Saving Bonus round closed (part 8). Commonwealth Games cancellation: more than $589m for an event that did not happen (part 5).

## 5. Federal and local boundaries

The Commonwealth sets the GST distribution and contributes to major projects ($6bn to SRL East); it runs the Capacity Investment Scheme the state's 2030 energy target depends on. Councils levy rates, are subject to the state's rate cap, and collect the emergency-services levy for the state. Rural Councils Victoria's 2026 platform, https://ruralcouncilsvictoria.org.au/vicvotes2026/ (live), asks for a $1bn Rural and Regional Productivity and Growth Fund with at least 25% guaranteed for rural Victoria, plus separate road maintenance funding and an extension of the Safe Local Roads and Streets Program beyond 2027.
